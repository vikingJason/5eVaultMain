# Pathfinder 2e for D&D 5e Players
## A Fast Transition Cheat Sheet

Welcome to Pathfinder 2nd Edition!  
PF2e and D&D 5e share a lot of fantasy DNA, but they *play* very differently at the table.

The biggest adjustment:

> PF2e rewards teamwork, tactics, positioning, preparation, and using the right action at the right time.

This guide is designed to help experienced 5e players avoid common traps and feel effective quickly.

---

# The Biggest Differences

| D&D 5e | Pathfinder 2e |
|---|---|
| Advantage/Disadvantage | Circumstance bonuses/penalties and conditions |
| Action + Bonus Action + Movement | 3 Actions + 1 Reaction |
| Bounded accuracy | Tight math scaling |
| Builds mostly defined by class | Builds defined by feats and choices |
| Multiattack scales automatically | Every attack after the first gets penalties |
| HP attrition expected | Tactical combat expected |
| Individual heroics | Team synergy is king |
| GM often improvises rules | PF2e has explicit rules for most things |

---

# The Golden Rule of PF2e

## +1 Matters. A LOT.

In 5e, a +1 bonus is nice.

In PF2e, a +1 bonus can:
- Increase hit chance
- Increase crit chance
- Prevent enemy crits
- Stack with ally support

Because critical hits happen when you exceed a DC by 10, every modifier matters.

### Example

- AC 20 enemy
- Roll total 20 = hit
- Roll total 30 = critical hit

That means:
- Flanking
- Frightened
- Buff spells
- Aid
- Debuffs

...all dramatically improve party performance.

# The 3-Action Economy

Every turn you get:
- 3 Actions
- 1 Reaction

Most things cost 1 action.

## Common Actions

| Action                         | Cost |
| ------------------------------ | ---- |
| Strike (attack)                | 1    |
| Stride (move)                  | 1    |
| Raise Shield                   | 1    |
| Demoralize                     | 1    |
| Recall Knowledge               | 1    |
| Interact (draw item/open door) | 1    |
| Cast many spells               | 2    |
| Some powerful abilities        | 2–3  |

# Multiple Attack Penalty (MAP)

This is the #1 thing 5e players struggle with.

## Attacking repeatedly gets worse.

| Attack Number | Penalty |
|---|---|
| First attack | No penalty |
| Second attack | -5 |
| Third attack | -10 |

Agile weapons reduce this to:
- -4
- -8

## IMPORTANT

Your third attack is usually the weakest possible use of an action.

Instead of attacking 3 times:
- Move
- Feint
- Demoralize
- Raise Shield
- Recall Knowledge
- Aid an ally
- Take cover
- Trip/Shove/Grapple

PF2e rewards tactical variety.

# Critical Hits & Critical Failures

## Critical Success

You critically succeed when:
- You beat the DC by 10+
OR
- Roll a natural 20 (usually improves success by one degree)

## Critical Failure

You critically fail when:
- You miss by 10+
OR
- Roll a natural 1 (usually worsens result by one degree)

This applies to:
- Attacks
- Saving throws
- Skills
- Spells
- EVERYTHING

This makes buffs and debuffs extremely important.

---

# Four Degrees of Success

Most checks have:

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Critical Success | Amazing |
| Success | Good |
| Failure | Bad |
| Critical Failure | Very bad |

Spells especially use this system.

### Example

- Fireball may still deal half damage on success
- Some spells become devastating on critical failure

# Teamwork is Mandatory

5e often allows players to independently optimize.

PF2e assumes:
- Frontliners create openings
- Casters support allies
- Debuffs matter
- Positioning matters
- Skills matter in combat

## Strong PF2e teamwork examples

### Martial Setup
1. Fighter Trips enemy
2. Rogue attacks Off-Guard target
3. Cleric buffs fighter
4. Bard demoralizes enemy

That stack of small bonuses becomes huge.

---

# Conditions Matter More Than Damage

In PF2e:
- Frightened
- Off-Guard
- Slowed
- Stunned
- Sickened

...can win encounters.

## Key Conditions

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Frightened | Penalty to almost everything |
| Off-Guard | Lower AC |
| Slowed | Lose actions |
| Stunned | Lose actions and reactions |
| Sickened | Penalties + action tax |
| Grabbed | Immobilized and vulnerable |

Removing enemy actions is extremely powerful.

---

# Movement Matters More

In 5e:
- Opportunity attacks discourage movement

In PF2e:
- Reactions are rarer
- Movement is more fluid
- Positioning matters constantly

You should:
- Reposition often
- Flank enemies
- Break line of sight
- Use terrain
- Retreat when needed

Standing still and full-attacking is usually suboptimal.

---

# Healing Works Differently

## Out-of-combat healing is expected.

PF2e assumes the party usually starts fights near full HP.

Main sources:
- Treat Wounds
- Battle Medicine
- Healing spells
- Focus spells

The Medicine skill is extremely valuable.

A party without healing support will struggle.

---

# Shields Are ACTUALLY Good

In PF2e:
- Raising a shield costs an action
- Shields give AC bonuses
- Some classes can Shield Block

A shield user can become dramatically tougher.

---

# Casters Play Differently

This is one of the biggest mindset shifts.

## Casters are NOT encounter-ending gods.

PF2e casters excel at:
- Area damage
- Buffs
- Debuffs
- Battlefield control
- Utility
- Exploiting weaknesses

Martials are consistently excellent at single-target damage.

Casters are strongest when:
- Targeting weak saves
- Applying conditions
- Supporting allies
- Adapting to encounters

---

# Recall Knowledge Is Incredible

Use Recall Knowledge constantly.

Learning:
- Weak saves
- Resistances
- Weaknesses
- Special abilities

...can completely change encounters.

A smart party often outperforms a high-damage party.

---

# Skills Are Useful in Combat

Unlike many 5e games, PF2e expects skill usage during combat.

## Excellent combat skill actions

| Skill | Combat Use |
|---|---|
| Intimidation | Demoralize |
| Athletics | Trip, Grapple, Shove |
| Deception | Feint |
| Diplomacy | Bon Mot |
| Medicine | Battle Medicine |
| Acrobatics | Tumble Through |
| Arcana/Nature/etc | Recall Knowledge |

---

# Character Building Philosophy

## PF2e rewards specialization.

Trying to be good at everything is weaker.

Characters usually excel by:
- Picking a role
- Supporting party synergy
- Building around strengths

## Your class matters less than your feat choices.

You gain:
- Class feats
- Skill feats
- Ancestry feats
- General feats

This creates massive customization.

---

# Difficulty Is Different

PF2e encounters are generally:
- More tactical
- More dangerous
- More mathematically balanced

A Severe encounter can absolutely kill reckless parties.

## Common rookie mistakes

### BAD:
- Standing still attacking 3 times
- Ignoring conditions
- Ignoring positioning
- Splitting the party
- Not healing between fights
- Not supporting allies

### GOOD:
- Flanking
- Buffing
- Debuffing
- Repositioning
- Using Recall Knowledge
- Helping allies crit
- Removing enemy actions

# Things 5e Players Usually Love About PF2e

## Martials feel AMAZING

Fighters, Rogues, Rangers, Monks, Champions, etc:
- Have meaningful choices every turn
- Scale well
- Feel tactical and powerful
- Get cool combat actions constantly

## Encounter balance works

A boss actually feels like a boss.

## Character customization is incredible

Very few characters feel identical.

## The GM has to improvise less

PF2e has robust rules support.

# Beginner Advice by Role

## Frontliners
- Flank constantly
- Protect allies
- Use Athletics actions
- Move strategically
- Raise shields when possible

## Rogues/Skill Characters
- Create Off-Guard opportunities
- Use skills constantly
- Exploit enemy weaknesses
- Avoid standing toe-to-toe

## Casters
- Learn enemy saves
- Use buffs/debuffs
- Don’t just spam damage
- Save top spells for big moments

## Support Characters
- You are EXTREMELY valuable
- Buffs win fights
- Healing prevents crits
- Action economy manipulation is king

# Suggested First-Time Player Mindset

## Don’t try to play PF2e like optimized 5e.

Instead:
- Think tactically
- Use teamwork
- Move often
- Use skills
- Support allies
- Exploit weaknesses

PF2e rewards clever play far more consistently than brute force.

---

# Fast Turn Checklist

## Before your turn
- Who needs help?
- Which enemy is weakest?
- Can you create Off-Guard?
- Can you buff/debuff?

## During your turn
- First attack is strongest
- Move intelligently
- Use skills
- Protect allies
- Don’t waste actions

## After your turn
- Did you set up the next player?

That last question is often the most important one in Pathfinder 2e.

# Final Advice

Your first few sessions may feel:
- More tactical
- More difficult
- More rules-heavy

That’s normal.

Once the system clicks, PF2e becomes:
- Deeply strategic
- Extremely rewarding
- Highly customizable
- Wonderfully cooperative

And yes...

Eventually someone in the party *will* discover the joy of critting because the Bard frightened the enemy, the Fighter flanked it, and the Cleric buffed everyone.

That moment is pure Pathfinder.

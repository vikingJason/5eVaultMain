# 25 Best Uncommon Magic Items In D&D 5e


In particular, *D&D 5e*'s Uncommon magic items have plenty to offer players. Although they're often loot found and used in the early stages of a campaign, Uncommon items will remain party mainstays through to a campaign's end. Some stand out above others for how invaluable they are, which still rings true with the recent *D&D 5e* 2024 version.

***Updated on January 20th, 2025 by Louis Kemner:*** *Many players want* D&D 5e*'s best Legendary magic items as soon as possible. However, the lower rarities are invaluable in their own right. This list has been updated with five more entries for players' reference, including items players will want to try out in any campaign or one-shot. Such items include noteworthy additions from the* D&D 5e *2024 revision.*

## 25. Emerald Pen Can Write Sneaky Messages That Hide In Plain Sight

### Fizban's Treasury of Dragons, pg. 23

The Emerald Pen is a magic item introduced in *Fizban's Treasury of Dragons*. It has a niche but fun ability that more creative *D&D* parties are sure to get some use out of. Besides writing without ink, a character holding the Emerald Pen can use it to cast the Illusory Script spell at will. Illusory Script allows the writer to encode messages so only a few others can read them.

This can be used to great effect in a *D&D 5e* campaign focused on intrigue. Even better, the Emerald Pen doesn't require attunement as a magical item. Most likely, the Emerald Pen will be used by a scholarly wizard character to encode their precious notes while studying the arcane, or a clever rogue might use the Emerald Pen to send covert messages to an ally without worrying about hostile NPCs intercepting and reading them.


## 24. Any Enhanced Spellcasting Focus Will Make Casters That Much More Potent

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014) & Tasha's Cauldron of Everything

![[sorcerer.png]]

There is no universal spellcasting focus magic item in *D&D 5e*. Instead, every spellcasting class has its own. They range from the warlock's Rod of the Pact Keeper in the 2014 *Dungeon Master's Guide* to items like the wizard's Arcane Grimoire or the sorcerer's Bloodwell Vial. The Uncommon forms of these items give a +1 to spell attack rolls and saving throw DCs. This is more significant than a +1 to weapon attacks and damage. Spells can target multiple creatures and have more devastating effects than mere damage.

Any increase in saving throw DC increases a spellcaster's chances of taking one or more enemies out of the fight. These tools are some of the best Uncommon magic items in *D&D 5e* for any spellcaster. Although it's such a basic, obvious item, some players might forget to upgrade their spellcasting focus. Players might take it for granted that their wizard or cleric can sling spells, neglecting the fact that a physical item is always involved. It's an example of why players should carefully review every detail of their character sheet and make sure everything is optimized.

## 23. A Driftglobe Will Illuminate Any Dungeon

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), pg. 254

One of the most basic but pervasive obstacles in any dungeon complex or cave network is the sheer darkness found inside. Species such as tabaxi and deep gnomes can see in the dark just fine. However, it's best if the entire party can see, so communal light sources like the driftglobe are a must.

Characters often have torches in their inventory, but driftglobes are fancier. When used, **a driftglobe will give off light equal to either the Light or Daylight spells** and will last much longer than a torch. Such light can impose disadvantage on light-averse hostiles like drow, allowing the party to gain the upper hand while exploring hazardous realms like the Underdark.

## 22. Gloves of Missile Snaring Are Ideal for Shutting Down Enemy Archers

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 172

The Gloves of Missile Snaring is an iconic *D&D* magic item that has saved many characters' lives. When wearing them, a character can use their reaction to deflect a ranged weapon attack sent their way. It reduces the damage by 1d10 plus a character's Dexterity modifier. This can negate the damage from an arrow or javelin, and even take the bite out of a ballista bolt. The Gloves of Missile Snaring provide consistent protection from ranged attacks.

Even better, they don't require attunement or charges. A *D&D* character can take this reaction any number of times in an adventuring day. Particularly in adventures against more mundane opponents, they can protect players from dozens or even hundreds of points of damage. They're one of *D&D 5e*'s best Uncommon magic items for defense. Players should note that the monk class has the Deflect Missiles class feature, making Gloves of Missile Snaring redundant when building powerful D&D monk characters. So, it's usually other martial characters who would want these gloves.

## 21. Gem of Brightness Can Blind Enemies With Ease

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 171

The best Uncommon magic items in *D&D* serve multiple purposes. Plenty of magic items exist to provide light, but none do it with the versatility of the Gem of Brightness. This small prism has 50 charges and can be used to cast light in a radius slightly greater than that of a torch. However, the Gem of Brightness's usefulness doesn't stop there.

The bearer of the Gem of Brightness can expend charges to make attacks that will blind targets, either one at a time or in a wide cone. Blindness is one of D&D 5e's most devastating status effects, especially against spellcasters. The Gem of Blindness cannot regain charges and will eventually run out. Before then, however, it's invaluable both in and out of combat.

## 20. Dust of Disappearance Turns the Whole Team Invisible

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), pg. 255


For stealth, the party has a few options, such as sending out the light-footed rogue and using Disguise Self or even having a changeling member of the team. Alternatively, characters can rely on invisibility, which is the best way to slip past any hostile creatures. Spellcasters can cast spells to that effect, or the party can use an item like Dust of Disappearance.

The Cloak of Invisibility is indeed the best item for steath purposes, but it only affects one character and is difficult to find. At lower levels, a *D&D* party is more likely to find and use the Dust of Disappearance than the Cloak of Invisibility, which can be sprinkled onto the entire team if everyone is huddled up together. The characters will then turn invisible for 2d4 minutes, and they must take care to not attack anyone or cast a spell before they're finished with that stealth.

## 19. Nature's Mantle Boosts the Stealth of Rangers and Druids

### Tasha's Cauldron of Everything, pg. 133


Nature's Mantle is an item specifically for rangers and druids introduced in *Tasha's Cauldron of Everything*. One of its most notable features improves a character's stealth capabilities. It lets them take the Hide action even if they're being directly observed, as long as they are lightly obscured. They can disappear before a creature's eyes in fog, smoke, or even dim light.

This is invaluable for any character. However, Nature's Mantle has more. It also functions as a spellcasting focus when worn. This is less necessary for druids, who often dedicate a hand to a focus or component pouch. However, it's invaluable for *D&D* rangers who often dual-wield weapons. It lets them cast without having to fiddle with their gear.

## 18. Cloak of Protection Is Ideal "Armor" for Squishy Spellcasters

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 159

![[Cloak of Protection.png]]

A Cloak of Protection has a straightforward but very notable effect. It provides a +1 bonus to Armor Class and all saving throws. Every single *D&D 5e* character will benefit from being harder to hit and more resistant to spells. In particular, the Cloak of Protection isn't armor, so even spellcasters and other fragile characters can wear it and be that much tougher in combat.

The Cloak of Protection continues to be beneficial throughout an entire *D&D 5e* campaign, particularly as its Armor Class bonus can stack with improvements from other items. Its general defensive properties make the Cloak of Protection one of the best Uncommon magic items in *D&D 5e*, and since it's relatively cheap and easy to find, the party might end up having a Cloak of Protection on two or even three characters at once. At higher levels, though, different classes will opt for fancier robes to up their game, such as Robe of the Archmagi at the legendary rarity.

## 17. Elemental Gems Summon a Meat Shield for Their Users

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 167

The Elemental Gems are a set of Uncommon magic items that offer a wildly powerful effect for their relatively low level. There are four variations, one for each different elemental type: fire, water, air, and earth. When shattered, they summon a *D&D* elemental of their corresponding kind. This summoning follows the same rules as the Conjure Elemental spell, meaning the elemental is nominally under the summoner's control.

However, if the conjurer loses Concentration, the elemental turns on them. This makes an Elemental Gem a high-risk, high-reward item at lower levels best saved for emergencies. The Elemental Gems are best used during a tough fight if the party needs a disposable monster to serve as a bullet sponge or keep a dangerous enemy busy. It's especially good for squishy casters who need a meat shield to keep the heat off of them.

## 16. Decanter of Endless Water Provides Water Whenever It's Needed

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 161

The Decanter of Endless Water is one of the best Uncommon magic items in *D&D* *5e* due to its sheer versatility. This non-attunement item can produce near-infinite amounts of water. When the Decanter of Endless Water is uncorked, it can trickle out in a stream (about one gallon's worth per turn), a fountain (five gallons per turn), or a geyser (generating 30 gallons).

These effects aren't game-changing in their own right. However, they also aren't limited by charges or attunement. The Decanter of Endless Water trivializes one aspect of resource management. Furthermore, canny players can find other uses for that much water. One example is for survival's sake, such as if the party is trekking across a desert.

## 15. Weapon of Warning Makes Initiative Rolls Easy

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), pg. 324

Some classes in *D&D* care a great deal about going first in battle, meaning they will seek out items to enhance their initiative rolls. Defensive items like the Sentinel Shield can help with that, but not every character wants to or even can use a shield. Those characters can use a Weapon of Warning, which can take many forms, including shortswords, greatswords, warhammers, and much more.

**A Weapon of Warning will grant the wielder advantage on their initiative rolls**, which is vital for a barbarian, who will want to use their Rage feature right away to get their defenses up. Also, a supporting character like a cleric can use the Weapon of Warning to make sure they go early in the battle to cast buff spells like Bless.

## 14. +1 Weapons Provide Decent Stat Boosts and Can Harm Any Foe

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 213

Weapons are some of the most prolific magic items in *D&D 5e*. The game has many swords, daggers, and more with spectacular additional effects. However, a plain +1 weapon is often all a character needs. It doesn't drain life from its enemies or roll extra damage dice; it does what a regular weapon does, but much more effectively.

A +1 weapon is the equivalent of an Ability Score Improvement in a character's primary stat. It also allows a character to exceed the typical caps on attack bonuses and damage. Its most valuable ability is letting a martial D&D player character ignore enemy resistances or immunities to non-physical damage. Although there are more exciting magic weapons, a +1 is reliable for parties at a low level until they are ready for +2—or even +3 weapons—later in the campaign.

## 13. Dust of Dryness Eliminates Water on Command

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), ptg. 166

At higher levels, D&D items' uses become obvious regarding how to use them. By contrast, Common and Uncommon items often require some creative thinking. Dust of Dryness can be used to compress a large area of water into a tiny pellet, potentially proving very useful for navigating flooded or swampy regions.

The pellet created by the Dust of Dryness can be shattered later to release the water, a strange but versatile feature for players who like to use their items to solve obscure problems. The Dust also has a much more situational secondary use. It deals high damage to creatures mostly composed of water, such as water elementals.

## 12. Pipes of Haunting Scare Enemies to Demoralize Them In Battle

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 185

D&D has plenty of magic instruments. However, most of them can only be attuned by a bard. The Pipes of Haunting are one of the best non-attunement magic items in D&D 5e. They allow any character proficient with wind instruments to conjure their magic.

Playing the Pipes of Haunting creates a magical effect that frightens those who hear it. Frightening a creature is a powerful effect, as it gives them disadvantage on their attack rolls and ability checks while the source of their fear is present. The DC for the saving throw on this effect is relatively high. Non-hostile creatures in the area can automatically pass the save, adding to the Pipes' benefits.

## 11. Keoghtom's Ointment is a Handy Healing Item

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), 324


Healing potions of varying strength are commonly found in *D&D*, using quite a few d4 to heal the drinker. If the party wants some variety, though, the DM can sprinkle in a few jars of Keoghtom's Ointment instead, which heals with different dice and can even rid the character of the poisoned condition. By contrast, healing potions only restore HP and do nothing else.

**A single use of Keoghtom's Ointment heals someone for 2d8 +2**, which is a decent amount on its own. But what's more, the Ointment can be used more than once. It will have 1d4 +1 doses, though a generous DM will probably set each one to the full five doses in a challenging dungeon if this item is found in the Tomb of Annihilation, for example.

## 10. Gauntlets of Ogre Power Are Great for the Party's Heavy-Hitters

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 171

The Gauntlets of Ogre Power don't provide a bonus in the conventional sense in *D&D 5e*. Instead, they set the wearer's Strength score to 19 if their Strength is normally below that. They have the same effect whether the character normally has 8 Strength or 18. This gives them two distinct uses.

Characters can use the Gauntlets of Ogre Power to enhance their Strength until they surpass 19 naturally and move onto a different magic item. Alternatively, a character can accept staying at 19 for the rest of the campaign and focus on other ability scores or feats. The Gauntlets of Ogre Power are one of the best Uncommon magic items in *D&D 5e* for their character-building potential.

## 9. Broom of Flying Grants Extra Mobility to the User

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 156

It cannot be overstated just how important the ability to fly is in *D&D*. Flying above combat removes the fear spellcasters and other fragile characters have of melee opponents. Most low-level enemies rely on melee attacks. Using this uncommon magic item is a great way to avoid that danger entirely. Or, the flying player character can get a high vantage point and scope out the terrain if climbing something tall, such as a tree, isn't an option. The Broom of Flying is also great in combat against a dragon, just in case a dragon flies out of the player's spell range.

At higher levels, flying becomes a reliable part of most characters' arsenals. A Broom of Flying early on can provide players with far more freedom to bypass obstacles than the DM is used to. A Broom of Flying's limitless amounts of flight per day makes it one of the most versatile, invaluable, and beloved magic items in all *D&D 5e*. Players should note that **in the 2024 rules, the Broom of Flying requires attunement.**

## 8. Boots of Elvenkind Push Rogue Stealth to the Next Level

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 155

*D&D 5e* features combat as one of its central mechanics. However, many characters prefer a subtler touch to their problems. The Boots of Elvenkind completely silence the wearer's footsteps and give advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks to remain unheard. They're one of the best Uncommon options for stealthy characters in *D&D 5e*.

Boots of Elvenkind help a character sneak past an enemy emplacement at night, move around unnoticed while invisible, or find new hiding places if the enemy knows where they are. They won't win combats, but not all of *D&D 5e*'s Uncommon magic items are meant to. Instead, they provide an invaluable way to avoid trouble in the first place.

## 7. Staff of the Adder Deals Poison Damage With Each Strike

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), pg. 310

Staffs may not be used that often as weapons, but if a low-level party comes across cool uncommon magic items like the Staff of the Adder, they might give these weapons a try. A Staff of the Adder is meant for direct strike attacks, dealing piercing as well as poison damage when the staff's serpentine head comes to life and bites the target. Notably, the staff can be directly attacked and destroyed while its head is animated.

The exact details of the Staff of the Adder vary from the 2014 to 2024 rules in *D&D*. Both versions deal 1d6 piercing damage on a hit and 3d6 poison damage as well, but the 2014 version calls for a CON saving throw to avoid the poison damage. Also, the 2024 version adds not just the wielder's proficiency bonus to the attacks, but also their Wisdom modifier. Overall, the 2024 version of the Staff of the Adder is much more potent.

## 6. Wand of Magic Missiles Is Great for Dealing Damage While Conserving Spell Slots

### Dungeon Master's Guide (2014), pg. 211

Wands are invaluable in *D&D 5e*. They allow characters to cast spells they don't normally have access to, even if they're not spellcasters themselves. Magic Missile is one of *D&D 5e*'s most dependable spells; it cannot miss or be avoided. Only a handful of abilities counteract it. It's one of the few abilities in the game that always deals its damage.

A Wand of Magic Missiles is one of the most valuable wands in *D&D*. It gives any character a reliable fallback option. It's a great Uncommon *D&D 5e* magic item for spellcasters who are out of spell slots, melee fighters who lack ranged damage, and any character without good damage options in their own right. More charges can be used to upcast, letting it deal respectable damage from a low level. Put another way, it's a fine endurance-based item for D&D casters who conserve their spell slots during a tough dungeon crawl.

## 5Adamantine Armor Nullifies All Critical Hits

### Basic Rules (2014)

![[Oath Breaker Paladin.png]]

The martial classes in *Dungeons & Dragons* usually wear medium or heavy armor, and for that matter, so do clerics, though clerics have the benefit of staying in the party's rear in most battles. There are many ways for armor to boost the wearer's defense, such as +1 to AC, but if the enemy does land a blow, the wearer will need some other form of protection.

That's where adamantine armor comes in, appearing as a variety of non-hide armor types, such as scale mail and splint, among other types. The effect is simple but useful: any critical hit dealt to the wearer becomes a normal hit, at no cost to the wearer. Critical hits may not be common for monsters, but if they do land, then adamantine armor ensures the stalwart fighter or paladin doesn't go down in just one or two hits.

## 4. Bracers of Archery Bulk Up a Ranged Attacker's Damage Rolls

### Basic Rules (2014)

It's true that martial *D&D 5e* characters already have access to items like +1 swords and +1 bows or crossbows, but there is still some appeal to items like the Bracers of Archery. For one thing, these items make the wearer proficient with shortbows and longbows, so anyone can become an instant archer when wearing them. Few other items can suddenly make a character proficient with anything.

The right armor in D&D can make all the difference in an adventuring party's success, and when that armor is magical, it makes survival even easier.

The main draw of these bracers is that they grant a +2 damage buff to all damage rolls with a shortbow or longbow, which will add up pretty quickly when the archer in question can attack twice in a single action. While uncommon bows grant a bonus to both attack and damage rolls, it's only a +1 bonus, while Bracers of Archery forgoes attack bonuses in exchange for shoring up the damage roll. For players who love cranking out lots of damage, that trade is worth it.

## 3. Goggles of Night Makes Night Missions Easy

### Basic Rules (2014)

A remarkable variety of playable fantasy species already have darkvision in *D&D 5e*, ranging from the feline tabaxi to the owlin and certainly deep gnomes, among others. Such people are ideal for night missions, but they might have the wrong class or party role for that, so items like Goggles of Night can help bridge the gap.

If the party's rogue, ranger, or scout lacks darkvision, then Goggles of Night will fix everything. The wearer will gain 60 feet of darkvision, so it'll be a cinch to scout an enemy base at nighttime or scope out a pitch-dark dungeon to find traps or obstacles. Best of all, this item isn't redundant with darkvision-having characters. When worn, these goggles will boost the character's existing darkvision by another 60 feet.

## 2. Headband of Intellect is Ideal for Low-Level Characters Focusing on Smarts

### Basic Rules (2014)

Intelligence is often neglected by most *D&D* characters since it is so rarely used for spellcasting or attacks, and players might even neglect it with their skills. Naturally, players tend to favor items boosting STR, DEX, and CON instead, such as the Amulet of Health. Still, some parties will want a Headband of Intellect, which overrides the wearer's INT score with 19.

This is an excellent item for both wizards and artificers, who use INT to make their spell attack, and a 19 is a fine way to get a +4 bonus. Eventually, this item may become obsolete, but at lower levels, it's a must-have for brainiac characters. In some cases, this may also help with an investigation, with skill rolls like Investigation, History, Religion, and Nature coming into play.

## 1. Pearl of Power Makes Spell Slot Management a Trivial Affair

### Basic Rules (2014)

The Pearl of Power is one of the most universally useful items in all of *D&D*, most of all when the party is at lower levels. This item will eventually feel underwhelming, such as at level 10+, but until then, this little item will be any spellcaster's best friend. Its effect is simple: once per day, a spellcaster may restore one spell slot at 3rd level or lower.

Spell slot management is a serious matter for casters, especially for warlock characters, but the Pearl of Power can take the edge off. This helps a caster use more magic during a series of battles in a dungeon, and it allows someone to cast a free spell between battles, such as Mage Armor or Bless, and more. When the party finds a Pearl of Power, the spellcasters are bound to fight over it, so the DM is encouraged to add some other magic-inclined items to make sure every caster gets something they will need.

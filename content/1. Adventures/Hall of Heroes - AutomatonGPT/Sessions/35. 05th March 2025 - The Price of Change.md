## Key Events

**Lumday, 9th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 


## Summary


Rinji was not a creature of patience. While his companions busied themselves with matters he neither fully understood nor cared for, he slipped away, moving through the quiet streets toward Sylvaen’s Apothecary.  
<br><br>
He had business to attend to. Inside, the air was thick with the scent of dried herbs and alchemical powders, motes of dust swirling in the dim candlelight. Sylvaen stood there, her long fingers sifting through a collection of vials, her gaze lifting the moment he entered.  
<br><br>
Her expression told him everything.   
<br><br>
“It’s not ready.”  
<br><br>
Rinji’s tail flicked, the faintest sound of irritation.  
<br><br>
“I need one last ingredient,” she admitted. “A crystallized Kobold scale.”  
<br><br>
Rinji’s grin split his face before she even finished. He reached up, plucked a single scale from his arm, and held it out.  
<br><br>
Glitter.  
<br><br>
A flood of it. 
<br><br>
The moment the scale left his skin, iridescent dust poured from the tiny gap it left behind, shimmering like a dying star.  
<br><br>
Sylvaen arched an eyebrow. “Well… it’s certainly something.”  
<br><br>
She tapped a finger against her chin, thoughtful, and offered an alternative. Fey Nectar. She revealed it can mimic the properties of just about anything, but the results would be... unstable. That was a word that intrigued Rinji rather than deterred him. Rinji nodded without hesitation.
<br><br>
Sylvaen hesitated, but nodded and told Rinji to come back in the morning. Rinji left the shop, tail flicking, eyes bright.  
<br><br>
He returned to The Troll’s Tollbooth, his thoughts spiralled through his mind like a storm bottled tight, contained but ready to burst free at the right moment. His companions were elsewhere, entangled in their own affairs, but Rinji’s thoughts were consumed by his secret purpose. And so he sat at his table. Unmoving. Unblinking.  
<br><br>
The night stretched before him, endless and heavy. Behind the bar, Martha watched. She didn’t ask questions. Didn’t pry. But she waited. Every breath Rinji took, every shift of his fingers, every flick of his ear—she was ready, hoping for a request she could fulfil. 
<br><br>
But Rinji remained silent, a statue of determination, until the first rays of sunlight pierced the horizon and crept through the door as the rest of the party returned.  
<br><br>
That was his signal. In an instant, Rinji moved. Chair scraping back, tail swishing, he bolted for the door with all the eagerness of a beast released from its cage. Determination pulsed from him, felt in the pounding of his footsteps against the ground.  
<br><br>
Achroteth and Lullaby didn’t need to exchange words. They simply followed.  
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
Cedrick sighed as he leaned against the bar, rubbing a hand over his face. He was thirsty. Hungry. And eager to start work on his automatons, or his goggles.
<br><br>
So much to do, so many things to tinker with. But first he headed to the post office to pen an E'mail to his mentor Dorgle, requesting assistance with deciphering the blueprints he had received as a gift.
<br><br>
He returned to his room at the tavern and began working on an crafting an apron, honing his skills with the needle and thread.
<br><br>
---  
<br><br>
Sylvaen greeted Rinji with a smile that was equal parts pride and trepidation when he arrived at her door. The potion she handed him shimmered with an otherworldly light, its contents swirling as though alive. Her fingers lingered on the bottle’s neck, the weight of uncertainty in her stance. She warned him again of its instability, her curiosity piqued but her caution evident.
<br><br>
She did not ask if he was sure. They both knew the answer. Rinji tilted the vial, watching the liquid swirl. Achroteth intervened before Rinji could drink it on the spot, guiding him to the edge of town, away from prying eyes and potential collateral damage.
<br><br>
Rinji raised the vial. Licked his lips. And drank.  
<br><br>
The world tilted.  
<br><br>
His limbs grew heavy. His vision blurred. And then—  
<br><br>
Darkness.  
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
When Rinji’s eyes opened, the world around him was unfamiliar yet strangely comforting. He found himself in a cavern so vast that its ceiling seemed to dissolve into infinity, a dim, ambient light illuminating the endless expanse. Moss-covered rocks dotted the landscape, their soft green hues a stark contrast to the cavern’s otherwise otherworldly atmosphere. It was a place that felt both alien and like home, a paradox that resonated deep within him.
<br><br>
Rinji began to explore, his footsteps echoing in the stillness. He chose a direction at random and walked, the passage of time losing meaning as the cavern stretched on, seemingly endless. Each step brought him deeper into the unknown, yet he felt no fear. This was a journey he had been preparing for, though he hadn’t known it until now. And as he walked, the cavern seemed to breathe around him, its secrets waiting to be uncovered.
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
Lullaby and Achroteth stood watch over Rinji, their expressions a mix of curiosity and mild concern. The kobold twitched in his trance, his limbs jerking as if chasing something in a dream. His snout twitched, a low growl in his throat—like a puppy caught in the throes of some grand imaginary hunt.
<br><br>
Lullaby, never one to let an opportunity pass, leaned toward Achroteth. “You know, I think we should take advantage of this moment.”
<br><br>
Achroteth, unblinking, folded his arms. “No.”
<br><br>
“But I haven’t even said what I—”
<br><br>
“No.”
<br><br>
Lullaby rolled their eyes, then, with an exaggerated shrug, flung a handful of glitter over Rinji’s sleeping form. The particles caught the moonlight, swirling like tiny stars before settling into his scales.
<br><br>
Achroteth sighed.
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
Rinji’s eyes were drawn to the sky aflame with falling stars.
<br><br>
The meteors streaked above, leaving luminous trails that flickered out into the endless void. He walked, drawn forward by something unseen, the ground beneath him cool and damp. The cave stretched impossibly far in every direction—vast, timeless, paradoxically infinite yet contained.
<br><br>
A lake appeared before him, its surface an undisturbed mirror. Fireflies bobbed above the water like living embers, their golden light casting strange shapes onto the rocks. Rinji stepped closer, his reflection rippling as his breath disturbed the stillness.
<br><br>
And then, he wasn’t alone. Kobolds—many of them—stood within the water’s surface. Their reflections met his, mirroring his movements but remaining otherwise motionless. He looked up. The shore was empty. Looked back. The kobolds were still there.
<br><br>
The hairs along his spine bristled.
<br><br>
“I know you’re there,” he called.
<br><br>
The reflections stirred. A hand touched his shoulder, gentle but firm. He turned to meet the gaze of an elder kobold—Skrizik, the storyteller of his youth.
<br><br>
“We are all Storytellers,” Skrizik said, his voice the sound of shifting leaves.
<br><br>
The others joined in, chanting in an ancient dialect of Draconic. Their voices intertwined, rising and falling in a harmony that hummed through Rinji’s bones.
<br><br>
“You are not meant to be here yet,” Skrizik continued. “You walk a path of stories, but destiny is no guarantee.”
<br><br>
The words sent a tremor through him. Skrizik began to reveal that the Prophecy of the Last Flame was an important part of the Stoyteller's journey.
<br><br>
Then the sky darkened. A shape loomed over the horizon, vast wings unfurling—black scales gleaming with malice. A dragon, massive and terrible, cut through the sky with singular purpose. The storytellers moved as one, stepping between Rinji and the beast, their stances firm.
<br><br>
Skrizik turned, a gnarled scepter in hand, its head adorned with a moss-covered gemstone. “Seek knowledge, young one.”
<br><br>
Before Rinji could ask what he meant, Skrizik swung the scepter.
<br><br>
And struck him on the head.
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
Rinji awoke with a gasp. The real world came rushing back—Achroteth’s watchful gaze, Lullaby’s smirk, the weight of something new and unfamiliar settling within his chest. Something was wrong. He tried to summon the familiar spark of rage that usually coiled within him, the wild fire that drove him forward. But it was gone.
<br><br>
Silence.
<br><br>
His claws dug into the dirt as he rose, his breath uneven. Then, out of the corner of his eye, he saw movement. A figure, just beyond the tree line. A kobold. The stranger didn’t move. Didn’t run. Simply watched. Rinji moved like a ghost, swift and silent. The other kobold flinched, as if surprised that Rinji had noticed him at all.
<br><br>
The kobold hesitated, but introduced himself as Vraxxis, another of the storytellers. There was something off about him. Not like the others. Not like Skrizik and the reflections in the water. His voice held weight, but no warmth. A flicker of something unreadable in Vraxxis’ gaze caught Rinji offguard before he suggested that Rinji find himself a book.
<br><br>
Rinji frowned. The suggestion seemed more like a demand, but he nodded in agreement before heading back to The Troll's Tollbooth
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
Cedrick was still famished and headed downstairs for some food. Martha's exhaustion was obvious, she apologised that she was waiting for Galax to show up before she would take any orders for food, but Cedrick saw that the chef was standing at his station ready to cook.
<br><br>
Cedrick looked around at the other hungry patrons and showed Martha that Galax was present. She apologised and took Cedrick's order, but before he had time to realise his mistake, a small surge of Wild Fey Magic burst through, changing Martha into a cactus.
<br><br>
Cedrick frowned. That wouldn’t do. Rolling up his sleeves, donned the apron he just made, tied it with a firm tug, and stepped into the fray. He found the stack of orders that had gotten lost between the bar and the kitchen and rushed them through to Galax.
<br><br>
As soon as they were ready, the meals were delivered. Cedrick shot glances at the cactus between running plates. At this rate, he wondered if he’d even get the chance to start work on his Automaton.  
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
As Rinji moved toward The Troll’s Tollbooth, he did so with purpose. Lullaby and Achroteth followed, but neither spoke. They had seen him change. Felt the weight of something different in his presence.
<br><br>
What they hadn’t expected was Cedrick standing uncomfortably close to a cactus, looking as guilty as a child caught sneaking sweets.
<br><br>
Achroteth’s eyes narrowed. “You broke a Fey Law, didn’t you?” 
<br><br>
Lullaby flung themselves at the cactus, hugging the plant as a grieving parent would.
<br><br>
Cedrick cleared his throat. “Now, ‘broke’ is a strong—”
<br><br>
Achroteth raised a hand, muttered an incantation, and the cactus shimmered. A moment later, Martha stood in its place, arms crossed, eyes filled with the promise of retribution.
<br><br>
“...Misunderstanding,” Cedrick finished weakly.
<br><br>
Martha decided she had earned a drink. She took a Fire Breath with Butch and Cassidee.  
<br><br>
Then another.  
<br><br>
And another.  
<br><br>
Rinji dropped into a chair, rubbing his temples. “Something’s wrong.”
<br><br>
The others turned to him. He exhaled and shared that he could not  feel his rage.
<br><br>
The words settled over the group like a thick fog. Achroteth straightened, his expression unreadable. Rinji hesitated before confessing that he could now also feel something else that was foreign to him, empathy. Lullaby made a choking sound. Achroteth paused in contemplation.
<br><br>
Rinji, meanwhile, stared at the empty seat beside him. Murmuring. Listening. Lullaby and Achroteth exchanged glances. There was no one there.
<br><br>
Achroteth muttered something under his breath. Then, his fingers traced the air, magic pulsing outward. For a moment, his vision shifted, allowing him to see beyond the material world. He expected something obvious—a curse, a presence, an enchantment. But all he saw was a faint glow around Rinji, as if something unseen had settled within him.
<br><br>
They made their way to Town Hall in a hurry, Rinji eager to obtain a book for himself.
<br><br>
Glendelle looked as though she hadn’t slept in days, dark circles under her eyes as she scurried past them. The Council, meanwhile, had just emerged from yet another meeting, their expressions tight with frustration.
<br><br>
Lilareth barely had time to greet them before Rinji stepped forward, eyes burning with conviction. “I need a book.”
<br><br>
Lilareth raised an eyebrow. “A book?”
<br><br>
“A tome,” Rinji clarified. “A blank one. Large. Sturdy.”
<br><br>
Something in Lilareth’s expression shifted. A flicker of recognition.
<br><br>
Without a word, she led them to the library, moving past endless shelves of books until she reached a section near the back. Then, to their surprise, she reached out—not for a book, but for the entire shelf. With a firm tug, the shelf swung outward, revealing a hidden alcove lined with blank tomes.
<br><br>
She scaled a ladder, selecting one with care: a burnt amber cover, thick and worn, its pages untouched.
<br><br>
“This one,” she said, handing it down to Rinji. “Five hundred twenty-six pages.”
<br><br>
He took it, running a claw over the leather.
<br><br>
Outside, the rhythmic march of automatons echoed through the streets. Achroteth turned toward the sound, frowning.
<br><br>
Lilareth explained that with the Greencloaks disbanded, the Council has employed the automatons as guards. If anything were to happen to Finroth, it would throw us the town into an administrative nightmare that they were not prepared or equipped for.
<br><br>
Rinji barely heard her. His fingers tightened around the book.
<br><br>
A Storyteller needed a tome.
<br><br>
And for the first time in his life, he realized—he had a story worth telling.
<br><br>

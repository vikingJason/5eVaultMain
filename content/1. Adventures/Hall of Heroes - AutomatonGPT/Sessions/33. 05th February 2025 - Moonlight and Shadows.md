## Key Events

Achroteth pressed his hands against the fractured wall, weaving magic into the break, willing the stone to heal, to mend. Power thrummed through his veins, ancient and deliberate. He did not know if the goddess was watching, if she cared, but he had to try.  The stone shifted. Cracks sealed. And with it the guardians stopped.  

A silence settled over the room temple, heavy with unspoken meaning. The adventurers exchanged glances, uncertain, breathless, alive. Cedrick took the opportunity to retreat, returning to the treasury to retrieve a potion. He drank deep, the warmth of healing magic flowing through him.  

Beyond the hall, the next chamber appeared barren—a small, empty room of nothing. No doors. No passageways. A dead end.  

That was a lie.  Lullaby reached out, fingers brushing against what should have been stone—but instead, their hand passed through. The wall was not real.  

They stepped forward, slipping through the illusion into another empty chamber. Lullaby again quickly saw through the ruse and disbursed the illusion to reveal a far grander chamber.  

Tapestries, frayed at the edges but still vibrant, draped the walls like whispers of the past. They spoke of Sehanine, of her blessings, of elves on a sacred pilgrimage beneath shifting moons. Phases of silver and shadow, eternity woven in cloth.  And the light.  

Moonlight streamed through gaps in the ceiling, thick and radiant, as if it carried the very breath of the divine. The beams swayed, pulsing with celestial energy, as if responding to something unseen.  

Lullaby stepped into the light.  

The doors shifted.  

Gone. Then elsewhere.  

It was a game. A test. A puzzle written in moonlight and faith.  

The others followed, stepping into the shifting glow.  

And then—  

She appeared.  

An elven woman manifested from nothing, her form half-substance, half-spirit. She wore flowing robes, adorned with symbols of Sehanine—the same robes Brother Varras wore.  

She spoke, her words were lyrical, elegant, and foreign. Fawn recognized the language, but the meaning danced just out of reach.  

Achroteth whispered a spell, magic tugging at the words, twisting them into something understandable.  

“Travelers, I hope you bear no ill to the goddess or her worshippers.”  

Her voice was calm, yet edged with warning.  

“The Moonstone belongs to Sehanine. Only those marked by her may take it.”  

Silence.  

Then, a revelation.  

“The last who sought to steal it is imprisoned in the heart of the temple.”  

Fawn stepped closer. The elven woman lowered her hood. Her name was Elysandrial. She was not just a spirit, not just a guardian, she was the warden.  

And she spoke not of a prisoner, but of something else. 

“The creature within is no longer what it once was,” she said. “It is a shadow. A hunger. A thing that once was someone, but is no longer.”  

The warning settled deep, cold and weighty.  

Then she raised a hand.  

A door materialized before them.  

Cedrick’s eyes traced the arcane runes inscribed along its edges. He recognized them. Glyphs and wards, not to lock something out… but to keep something in.  

The sanctum opened before them, vast and sacred. Pillars rose like silent sentinels, their surfaces etched with glyphs in ancient Elvish, untouched by time. The walls were adorned with mosaics of the moon’s phases. The floor held smaller lunar engravings, glowing in synchronized rhythm. A heartbeat. A pulse.

Within the room were dust-covered mirrors that seemed out of place. Positioned at odd angles, mounted on stands that could pivot and turn—yet misaligned, as if waiting to be set right.  

What dominated the sacred chamber was the altar. Moonlight streamed from above, striking small silver mirrors positioned on the altar and reflected with focused precision into the room.

But it was what stood upon the altar stood that held their attention, the object they sought, the Sacred Moonstone.

It sang. A soft, harmonic hum vibrated through the air, a melody felt rather than heard. A song of reverence. Of power.  

Something stirred the moment Fawn stepped forward. A ripple through reality, like the temple itself had been holding its breath. A shadow moved, it uncoiled from the darkness, a creature twisted by time and something far worse. A voice—low, bitter, venomous—slithered through the space between them.  

"Do you truly serve her, child?"  

Seralith.

Once a cleric. Once a believer. But now, something else. His form was fractured, the edges of his body flickering like a dying flame, held together by sheer hatred.  

Fawn stood firm, appealing to Seralith's sense of justice and trying to remind them that the gods are not answerable to mortals.

A slow breath. Then—laughter. Hollow. Dry.  

"You think I don’t know that?" His voice carried the weight of prayers unanswered. "But tell me, Princess, when your own cries go unheard—when you watch the people you love crumble into dust before you—what will your faith mean then?"  

The tension in the chamber thickened.  

Achroteth, sensing the shift, wordlessly removed his shirt. The Dragonborn’s scales drank the light rather than reflecting it, the cosmic glow dimming against the dark expanse of his body. A living constellation. A void among stars.  

Cedrick stepped forward, his scholar’s mind pulling at the threads of the ritual chamber, searching for meaning in its every detail.  

Lullaby and Dorgle lingered at the threshold, hands twitching over the hilts of their weapons. Ready. Waiting.  

Something was going to happen.  

Something had to happen.  

A spark ignited in Fawn’s mind. She moved. Swift, decisive, certain. 

Dust swirled as she wiped clean a mirror beside her, angling its surface—catching the moonlight. She turned it, aligning the beam, bending Sehanine’s light toward the creature in the dark.  

The beam struck Seralith. He hissed. The reaction was instant—a recoil, a tremor in his form. Fawn’s lips curved into a knowing smile, Brother Varras had been right.

Seralith’s snarl cut through the stillness. He lunged, not to prove a point, but to teach a lesson.  

"Justice," he whispered, "isn’t a virtue, Princess. It’s punishment. And you—" He stepped forward, his presence stretching unnaturally in the dimming light. "You will know it. You will know what it means to hold someone you love in your arms as their final breath slips away."  

Achroteth’s form erupted into stars as he plunged into the chamber. Moonlight pulsed from him, cascading over the mosaics lining the walls. The ancient symbols, long dormant, responded.  

A wave of celestial energy surged outward. He felt it wrap around him, warm, strong—like faith made tangible.

Dorgle and Lullaby moved, the weight of the moment pressing them into action. They scattered into the chamber, searching, activating mosaics, pushing back against the dark.

Dorgle hesitated. His foot landed upon one of the lunar symbols, and a deep, consuming solitude crashed into him. A vision—of himself, utterly alone. No laughter. No companionship. Not even his shadow remained.

He staggered, breath shallow, hands trembling. But there was no time for fear. He reached for a final mirror, realigning it, angling the last piece of the puzzle.  
And the result was wrong. Instead of a radiant glow, the chamber was consumed, the light collapsed in on itself. Everything went black.

The silence was suffocating.

Seralith's rasping voice, slithered through the abyss.  

"Now you're all trapped in here with me."  

A breath.  

"And soon… you will know what fear truly means."

As the moonlight from his form shone over mosaics on the wall near him, a magic aura spread from the now glowing celestial body, he felt the a warm sense of strength and calmness.

Dorgle and Lullaby moved into the room, and everyone moved to quickly activate the mosaics in the hopes of sealing Seralith's fate.

Dorgle stepped onto a mosaic on the floor, and succumb to feelings of extreme loneliness, fearing what a life without even his shadow would look like.

Seralith proved to be a powerful adversary, taking the powerful magic and melee attacks in his stride.

Seralith's raspy voice filled the chamber, "Now you're all trapped in here with me! You will soon learn what fear means."

## Notes


The Sacred Moonstone of Sehanine is an intricately carved, milky-white gemstone in the shape of a crescent moon, its surface adorned with delicate etchings of swirling starlit skies and lunar phases. Embedded along its outer curve are tiny silver inlays resembling constellations, which shimmer faintly as if reflecting a night sky. When bathed in moonlight, the carvings seem to shift and dance, revealing fleeting glimpses of hidden truths and distant realms.

## Key Events

**Lumday, 9th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 
<br><br>
The Troll’s Tollbooth was quiet, save for the crackle of the fire in the hearth and the soft murmur of conversation. It had always been a place of warmth, where travelers and villagers alike gathered in the early evenings, seeking a brief respite from the world outside. Fawn stepped inside, the familiar scent of spiced ale and roasted meats greeting her.
<br><br>
The door hadn’t even closed behind her before the dynamic duo of Lullaby and Martha descended upon her. The two were like a whirlwind—sudden, inevitable, and hungry. But not for food. Oh no. They were after something else.
<br><br>
“Fawn!” Lullaby’s voice rang out, brighter than usual, as they practically dragged Martha to the table where Achroteth sat, already quiet and watching, the distant flicker of magic still dancing at the edge of his mind. “Come, sit! You must try this,” they said, gesturing grandly at the platter of meats and cheeses they had arranged.
<br><br>
Achroteth gave no response, merely watching the scene unfold through his eyes, half-lidded as if this was merely another fleeting moment in a long string of them. But Fawn noticed the flicker of curiosity in him as Lullaby presented their creation with a smile so wide it nearly split their face in two.
<br><br>
There was pride in Lullaby's eyes—genuine pride—an emotion that Fawn knew well. Lullaby had always been full of surprises. But there was something different today. A sense of achievement, perhaps even joy, bubbled up from the depths of their soul as they watched Martha and Fawn each take their first bite, savoring the unexpected combination of flavors that danced across their tongues. It was not just food, it was an offering, a declaration of skill. Lullaby beamed at them, standing there like a proud parent witnessing their child take their first steps.
<br><br>
Fawn tried to smile, but her mind was elsewhere. The temple’s remnants still echoed within her, an old magic that lingered in the back of her thoughts, waiting to be understood. She looked around the room, finding herself in an unfamiliar stillness. That was when the door swung open again.
<br><br>
Cedrick and Dorgle entered, carrying the unmistakable weight of a day spent in conflict. Cedrick barely spared a glance for the group before heading upstairs, a soldier retreating to the peace of his own thoughts. Dorgle, however, was a force of nature.
<br><br>
He sauntered in like a storm on the horizon, immediately setting his eyes on a table of villagers who had earned the right to unwind after a long day of labor. Dorgle’s gaze, sharp and predatory, locked onto them. Two of the villagers shrank beneath it, but one man—one man with three fingers—held Dorgle’s gaze unflinchingly.
<br><br>
It was a silent exchange, one that stretched for long moments. Dorgle’s mind raced as he took in the stranger. There was something about that smile—small, almost imperceptible, but defiant. Something in the way the man didn’t break.
<br><br>
An adversary.
<br><br>
Dorgle smirked, a quiet excitement building inside him. He wasn’t sure why, but something in that man’s smile struck a chord. It was a challenge—a test—and Dorgle never turned down a good fight, even if it was nothing more than a battle of wills.
<br><br>
But before he could continue his silent siege, a voice broke through the tension.
<br><br>
“Would you like to see something... interesting?” Achroteth asked, his voice as calm as the still waters of a pond.
<br><br>
Dorgle, curious and always ready for mischief, nodded without a second thought. Achroteth’s smile was the faintest flicker, but it held a promise.
<br><br>
The Druid’s hand landed lightly on Dorgle’s shoulder. A soft incantation. And then, in the space of a heartbeat, the Dwarf slumped forward. The mugs in his hands fell to the table, their contents sloshing, but the Dwarf’s body remained still—unnaturally still.
<br><br>
A gasp rang out. The room grew quieter. The air seemed to thin.
<br><br>
The magic was subtle, but its effect was immediate. To anyone watching, it looked as though Dorgle had suddenly dropped dead in the middle of his conversation. Achroteth did not react—he simply watched as the others scrambled to make sense of the bizarre scene. Feign Death, a spell that was as much about control as it was about spectacle.
<br><br>
Martha’s reaction, however, was the most telling. She had always been a complicated mix of bluntness and tenderness, but her face twisted into genuine concern as she knelt beside the fallen Dwarf. Her breath caught in her throat as she brushed a hand over his unmoving form. “I… I don’t hate him, you know,” she murmured, her voice a raw whisper, as if confessing a deep secret. “He’s infuriating, but... I’d never wish this on him.”
<br><br>
Achroteth, for all his quiet aloofness, looked down at her and offered a simple reassurance. “He will return. He always does.”
<br><br>
And true to Achroteth’s word, after a few moments had passed—enough time for the tension in the room to settle and for the crowd to get used to the oddness—Dorgle stirred. His eyes blinked open, confused, but only for a moment. The Dwarf looked around, disoriented, then glanced down at his hands, still holding the empty mugs. “What in the name of—?” he muttered, shaking his head and rubbing his temples.
<br><br>
He paused for a moment before his eyes returned to the three-fingered man, whose enigmatic smile remained unchanged.
<br><br>
The door chimed again, this time with an almost mechanical precision. The E-mail server entered the room, his purpose was simple: deliver messages. And he did so with impeccable accuracy.
<br><br>
Three envelopes materialized in the air before Fawn, each bearing familiar names. Calarel, Ness, and Celly. The weight of them, the anticipation, hit her all at once. She barely had time to glance at the writing before she burst out the door, unable to contain the rush of excitement that overwhelmed her.
<br><br>
“They’re here!” she called, her voice carrying into the evening air.
<br><br>
Achroteth raised an eyebrow, intrigued but not surprised. He moved to the kitchen, where Lullaby was still recovering from the intense moment with Dorgle. With a swift motion, he took them by the arm and led them outside, eager to discover what Fawn had seen.
<br><br>
Fawn’s explanation was simple, but it was enough. A convoy was approaching—one from the capital. It was just as she had hoped.
<br><br>
Achroteth didn’t speak. He didn’t need to. Instead, he stepped back, the light around him shifting, focusing, before his wings—a crystalline construct of light and magic—fanned outward. Lullaby clung to his back, a feeling of disorientation spiralling within them. But the sensation of flight quickly overwhelmed any sense of doubt.
<br><br>
They rose higher, higher still, until the town below was a patchwork of familiar streets and alleyways. The road ahead seemed clear, but there, in the distance, a cloud of dust rose from a long procession. A column of travellers—far off, but coming closer.
<br><br>
Fawn was already gone, headed toward Faebric Fashion to check on her dress. The anticipation in her footsteps was palpable.
<br><br>
Lullaby, still stunned by the sudden ascent, finally found their voice, their words coming out in a rush of disbelief. “What was that? What just happened?”
<br><br>
In their confusion, the magic within them twisted, a moment of instability. Feathers began to fall in uneven patches, leaving them with an oddly patchworked appearance.
<br><br>
Frustrated, they collapsed onto the ground, their emotions spilling out in a jumble of frustration and exhaustion.
<br><br>
Achroteth looked down at them, his gaze softening, and waited for the storm to pass. Lullaby always did this—they crumpled under pressure only to rise again, stronger and more resolute than before.
<br><br>
Once they had gathered themselves, they stood, brushing off the remnants of their disarray. Together, they joined Fawn at the town fountain, waiting for whatever was coming next.
<br><br>
The town itself seemed to hold its breath, waiting too.
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
The delicate chime of the bell above the tailor’s door announced Fawn’s entrance, the sound soft and musical against the quiet hum of the street behind her. The interior of Faebric Fashion was warm and scented faintly with lavender and wool. Spools of dyed thread lined the walls like a painter’s palette, and bolts of fabric shimmered under soft magical lights suspended from the rafters.
<br><br>
She hadn’t expected much—not yet. The timeline had been ambitious, the request a little unreasonable. But there they were. All three.
<br><br>
Thimble, Thread, and Tinsel stood proudly around three mannequins, each one adorned with a completed outfit. Fawn’s breath caught in her throat. She approached as if the garments were sacred relics.
<br><br>
The evening gown called to her.
<br><br>
A whisper of indigo silk, embroidered with delicate silver moths that shimmered like stars in moonlight. The bodice was stitched with precision beyond mortal patience, hugging her form in a way that felt both tailored and timeless. As she stepped into it, the world seemed to fall away. For a moment, Fawn wasn’t just a messenger, a puzzle-solver, or a pawn in a quiet war—she was royalty, she was power, she was grace itself.
<br><br>
She spun slowly in front of the mirror, the hem floating like a dream. Celly needed to see this. Fawn could already imagine the look on her face, the proud little smirk. The dress fit better than her own skin. She smiled, a rare, unguarded smile.
<br><br>
---
<br><br>
Dorgle was wrapped in suspicion and shadow. He prowled the streets of Petalwood like a wolf on the scent. The town bustled with pre-festival energy, but Dorgle’s focus was singular. He had his eyes locked on a man with only three fingers—a detail so small, yet it gnawed at the edge of his instincts.
<br><br>
Something about that man wasn’t right. The other villagers ignored him. The Automaton guards made their rounds with mechanical precision, their glassy eyes watching everything and nothing at once. But Dorgle paid them no mind. They weren’t his concern. The three-fingered man was. Always just ahead, always too calm.
<br><br>
And then the moment came. Dorgle surged forward, calling out to the man in a low growl.
<br><br>
The confrontation was sudden, jarring. Fawn—just returned from the tailor’s, her mind still swimming with satin and sentiment—tried to intercept him, stepping between the dwarf and the man. Her words were urgent, but fell on deaf ears. Dorgle was a storm now, and storms do not yield.
<br><br>
The Automaton guards reacted with speed and efficiency, stepping toward the conflict—but not fast enough. In one fluid, brutal motion, Dorgle drew steel and struck. The blade met flesh. The three-fingered man gasped, stumbled, and fell. The town square went still.
<br><br>
For a heartbeat, silence. Then, change.
<br><br>
The man’s form rippled. His features blurred like paint in water, melting into something alien. His skin became iridescent, his bones rearranged with sickening pops. Before the eyes of the town, the corpse transformed—revealing the truth.
<br><br>
A changeling.
<br><br>
Gasps and shouts rose from the gathering crowd, villagers stumbling back. And then, something stranger still. A pulse of arcane energy welled up from the changeling’s corpse—a crackling light with no known source. A single, glowing orb emerged from the body like a soul escaping, hovering for a moment in the air… before shooting straight into Dorgle’s chest.
<br><br>
He staggered, blinking, breathless—but still standing.
<br><br>
The Automaton guards immediately cordoned off the area, their movements swift and rehearsed, to begin a formal investigation.
<br><br>
Dorgle was questioned on the spot.
<br><br>
Unfazed, the dwarf straightened his shoulders and told them what he knew. “He was compromised. I’m with the CIA. This was a hostile infiltration. You’ll find my credentials… somewhere classified.”
<br><br>
The Automatons—utterly literal, strangely gullible—paused. Then one gave a slight nod.
<br><br>
“Assessment: Self-defence plausible. Threat confirmed. Investigation pending. You are advised not to leave Petalwood until final evaluation is complete.”
<br><br>
Dorgle grunted in agreement, the glow in his chest still faintly pulsing.
<br><br>
Behind him, the townspeople whispered in clusters. Fear and awe in equal measure. Petalwood had long held secrets. But now, with strangers walking among them, and the lines between reality and illusion growing thinner, something larger was beginning to stir.
<br><br>
And in the middle of it all, Dorgle stood—smirking faintly, one hand over his chest—wondering just what, exactly, had wormed its way inside him.


## Summary

After taking some time to reflect after the visiting the ancient temple, Fawn entered The Troll's Tollbooth and was simultaneously set upon by Lullaby and Martha, like starved vultures spotting the last morsel of flesh on a fresh carcass.

Lullaby, proud of their newly aquired skills, invited Martha to sit at the table with Fawn and Achroteth. Moments later they emerged from the kitchen with a platter of meats and cheeses, a smile booming with pride splayed across their face as they watched the table enjoy their handiwork.

Dorgle and Cedric entered the tavern after their journey back from their bought at the Fight Club. Cedrick headed upstairs to bed while Dorgle instantaneously began to employ his usual antics of trying to annoy the other patrons only to take pleasure in their reactions. The Dwarf spotted a group of villagers enjoying a well earned Ale and stared, causing two of them to shift nervously in their seat.

To Dorgle's surprise, one of the patrons did not react as he expected. Dorgle now considered the three fingered townsfolk an adversary, and tried to provoke further reaction which only resulted in a small smile.

Trying to best Dorgle at his own game, Achroteth asked Dorgle if he would like to see one of his newly acquired spells. The unassuming Dorgle agreed, causing a slight grin on Achroteth's face.

The Druid placed a hand on Dorgle's shoulder and cast Feign Death. Dorgle's body immediately slumped over in his chair, empty mugs in his hands, his chest gave no movement that confirmed he was breathing. To anyone who looked over it appeared that Dorgle had suddenly died. Achroteth was not concerned what anyone would think, he knew that secretly they would welcome the "break".

Martha, however, was concerned. She almost shed a tear when confronted with thought of Dorgle's death and admitted that she finds him infuriating but does not hate him and certainly would not wish death upon him. Achroteth assured Martha that Dorgle would be fine and that she had nothing to worry about.

After enough time had passed Achroteth woke Dorgle, the Dwarf having no recollection of what had happened went back to observing his new adversary.

The E-mail server arrived with three messages for Fawn, one from Calarel, Ness, and Celly. Fawn could not contain her excitement as she read them and burst out the door, yelling “they are here” on her way out. Achroteth headed to the kitchen and fetch Lullaby and the pair joined Fawn outside.

Fawn explained that there should be a convoy from the capital. Achroteth showed off his new crystal wings, taking flight with Lullaby attached to his back. Looking around from the high vantage point he did not see any evidence of a convoy of carriages in town but as he looked along the road he spotted a large column trailing dust in the distance, about an hours travel from town.

Unable to contain her patience, Fawn headed off  to [[Tailor - Faebric Fashions|Faebric Fashion]] to check on her dress. 

Lullaby, dazed from the unexpected jolt into the sky, questioned Achroteth about what had just happened. In doing so, they accidentally bestowed a curse on themself, feathers malted in uneven patches. Unable to deal with it, they threw themself into a heap on the ground. After collecting themself, Lullaby joined Achroteth in the main square near the town fountain. 

---

Fawn entered the tailor's store and was surprised to see that Thimble, Thread, and Tinsel, had finished all three of the outfits earlier than expected. Fawn tried on the evening dinner gown. It was breath taking, the detail was exquisite, the stitching was perfect, it fit better than anything Fawn had worn before.

Fawn couldn't wait to show Celly.

---

Meanwhile, Dorgle sleuthed through the streets of Petalwood, keeping an eye on the three fingered foe. Dorgle was sure that something was off about this person, something that no one else seemed to notice. Dorgle paid no mind to the Automaton's patrolling the streets, or the other villagers in the square. He moved in on his target, confronting him about his association with the CIA.

Fawn tried to stop him, but her efforts were unsuccessful.

The Automaton Guards intervened but Dorgle was too quick, he stabbed his foe and felled him immediately. As the corpse fell to the floor it began to change, transforming in front everyone in the town square and revealing that Dorgle's suspicions were correct. This was another changeling. Then, from the body of the changeling, energy began stirring and a strange glowing orb shot out and into Dorgle.

The automatons cornered off the area and began their investigation. They took a statement from Dorgle, who tried convincing them he was a member of the mysterious CIA. They bought the story, telling Dorgle that it seemed like a matter of self defence but not to leave Petalwood while they finalise their investigation.

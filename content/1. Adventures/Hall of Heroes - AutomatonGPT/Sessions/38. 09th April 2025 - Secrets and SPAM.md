## Key Events

**Lumday, 9th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 
</p><p></p><p>
The trees whispered.
</p><p>
Rinji stepped carefully beneath their boughs, the rich canopy filtering sunlight into molten shards of gold. His boots pressed softly into leaf-litter, his movements fluid but anxious—he was alone now, truly alone. The outskirts of Petalwood had always been quiet, but today, in this hollow formed by thick hedgerows and brambles, silence felt deliberate. Like the forest had made space for something sacred… or something secret.
</p><p>
He exhaled and set down his pack. A snap of his fingers, and the world shifted.
</p><p>
The earth responded—not as a passive thing, but like an obedient hound called to heel. Clods of dirt churned upward at the flick of his wrist. Lightning—his lightning—cracked through the air, not in defiance, but in play. Raw energy leapt from his fingertips and cracked a branch in two. It was exhilarating. Terrifying. Addictive.
</p><p>
“Oh, that was pathetic,” Vraxxis sneered.
</p><p>
The voice came from nowhere, yet everywhere—a ribbon of shadow at the corner of Rinji’s thoughts. The Kobold that had bound himself to Rinji’s power hovered just outside sight, as he always did, full of disdain and biting sarcasm.
</p><p>
Rinji gritted his teeth. “I didn’t ask for your commentary.”
</p><p>
“And yet you get it anyway.”
</p><p>
The tension snapped. Rinji lunged—not with power this time, but with muscle. He tackled the space where Vraxxis had last appeared. His arms closed around air, his body hitting the dirt in a graceless sprawl.
</p><p>
“You missed,” Vraxxis chuckled.
</p><p>
Face flush with irritation and soil, Rinji stood, dusted himself off, and gave the air an obscene gesture. Then, without another word, he turned back toward Petalwood.
</p><p>
By the time Rinji returned to the town square, something was happening. Of course, Dorgle was at the center of it. He always was.
</p><p>
The crowd shifted like a flock of startled birds. Murmurs rippled. Automaton guards lined the streets, their glowing eyes scanning for unrest. But the action had passed. Whatever chaos Dorgle had unleashed had already flared and faded.
</p><p>
Rinji said nothing. He merely found the Dwarf and fell in step beside him. There would be questions later, but for now, they walked together, heading toward the blacksmith.
</p><p>
Meanwhile, the Druid moved alone. Achroteth’s bare feet made no sound as he approached Petalwood’s modest town hall. Inside, Glendelle was pacing, her face a study in widening panic. 
</p><p>
“A caravan?” she repeated, wringing her hands. “From Glitterfall? But I didn’t… I mean, there’s nothing in the plans—nothing about—”
</p><p>
“Fawn may have taken initiative,” Achroteth offered, a calm boulder in the stream of her worry. “Perhaps extra tables, additional chairs...”
</p><p>
That did nothing to soothe her. “Finroth needs to know. Please, Achroteth. Let him know. He hates surprises.” 
</p><p>
Achroteth nodded once and slipped silently toward the mayor’s office. He paused at the door—not to knock, but to listen. A whisper met his ear. Soft, cracked. Full of mourning.
</p><p>
“Elara, I miss you. I’m doing this for you.”
</p><p>
Achroteth’s breath stilled. He knocked gently. The door opened slowly, revealing Finroth, seated behind his desk, a picture frame in his hands. His eyes were red-rimmed, his face weary. “There’s a convoy,” Achroteth said simply. “They’ll be arriving soon.”
</p><p>
Finroth looked up. Surprise flickered behind the grief. “From Glitterfall? That’s… unexpected.” Achroteth offered no further words. The moment was not for him. The door clicked closed as Achroteth left the Mayor's office. But Achroteth did not leave Town Hall.
</p><p>
Instead, he slipped into a shadowed alcove, and in the space between heartbeats, his form unraveled—fur retracting, limbs twisting, eyes multiplying. In seconds, the Druid became a spider no larger than a coin. He crept beneath the door.
</p><p>
The mayor was standing now, turned toward the grand window behind his desk. His back straightened. He whispered words—not of mourning this time, but of power. Magic rose from the floor, carving trails in the dust.
</p><p>
And then something fractured. A flash. A pulse. The spell rebounded, striking Finroth square in the chest. He staggered backward, groaning.
</p><p>
Achroteth clung to the top of the bookshelf, unmoving. Finroth opened a hidden drawer. From it, he withdrew a book bound in dull leather, etched with symbols. A spellbook. Old. Loved. Dangerous. He flipped through the pages, fingers trembling. The pendant around his neck clutched in his hand.
</p><p>
When Finroth finally left the room, Achroteth waited a moment longer. Then, he descended from the bookshelf, passed beneath the crack of the door, and shifted back to his elven form in the alley behind town hall. He did not speak. He merely returned to The Troll’s Tollbooth, escaped to his room, and withdrew a worn journal. With careful hands, he began to draw—sigils, glyphs, spirals—everything he had seen in the secret spellbook of Mayor Finroth. 
</p><p>
He did not yet understand what it meant.
</p><p>
But he would.
</p>
---
<p>
The forge was hot, as ever, and Lerendrie’s face glowed with more than just sweat. The old smith looked up from his bench, excitement catching like a spark in his eyes the moment he saw Dorgle and Rinji enter the workshop.
</p><p>
“Any word?” Lerendrie asked, wiping his hands on a cloth singed black with soot. “The permit—did you manage it?”
</p><p>
His voice carried the edge of desperation, carefully masked behind a craftsman's cheer. He wanted to believe in the system, but the people in Petalwood didn’t exactly put their faith in bureaucracy. Dorgle’s face twitched into a grin.
</p><p>
“Paperwork’s all but inked,” he said confidently, waving a hand as though dismissing some minor errand. “It’s being finalised as we speak. You'll be carving gems before the week is out.”
</p><p>
He said it with the ease of a man who had never filled out a form in his life, nor planned to. Lerendrie, to his credit, nodded with cautious optimism.
</p><p>
Rinji said nothing. He didn’t have to. His eyes narrowed the way they always did when Dorgle began spinning tales. He braced himself as Lerendrie listed prices for custom work—adjustments to their weapons, enchantment foci, and a few experimental modifications Rinji had requested for his armor.
</p><p>
Rinji winced pre-emptively. But then—surprise. Dorgle bartered.
</p><p>
Not shouted. Not threatened. He even—gods preserve us—negotiated.
</p><p>
Coins exchanged hands. The metal felt warm in Rinji’s palm as they left the shop, the faint scent of scorched brass clinging to their cloaks.
</p><p>
And then, they parted.
</p><p>
Dorgle’s boots thudded down the cobblestone toward the Post Office. “I sent a mass message,” Dorgle said. “Replies?”
</p><p>
The E-mail Server replied, “Your message was flagged under Code 451: SPAM. *Sorcerous Pestering by Arcane Messages*. Your account has been suspended.”
</p><p>
Dorgle blinked, “That’s... not ideal.” Thankfully he was able to open a new account. Dorgle never looked back. Only forward. But then came the form.
</p><p>
Long. Paper. Lines of runes. Boxes to tick. Dorgle’s eyes passed over the script like a man staring into a stormcloud—knowing danger was coming and lacking any ability to stop it.
</p><p>
“You appear... hesitant,” the Server said, suspicious now. “Verification is required.”
</p><p>
A glyph appeared midair. Spinning. Nonsensical. The E-mail Server requested that Dorgle complete the CAPTCHA: *Completely Arbitrary Pointless Trial of Curious Humanoid Aptitude*.
</p><p>
With a sigh that could've crumbled granite, Dorgle took the form and left—stuffing it into his coat pocket like it might dissolve if ignored long enough.
</p><p>
He would deal with it later. Probably. Or find someone else who could read.
</p><p>
For now, he made his way back toward the warm lights of The Troll’s Tollbooth, the tavern where stories began, and inevitably... ended.
</p>
---
<p>
The Troll’s Tollbooth was alive with color and conversation, the kind of harmony that only came when people felt safe enough to _relax_. Lullaby was hard at work in the kitchen, radiant. Their smile was less a smile and more a statement. A proclamation.
</p><p>
They had won.
</p><p>
In one corner of the tavern, a woman laughed into her drink, a coconut glass the color in hand with a slice of lime perched at an angle no natural fruit should hold. At another table, a pair of farmers clinked coconut and muttered their approval. The cocktail revolution had begun—and its architect was Lullaby.
</p><p>
They shared a glance with Gallax. He was wiping a spill with all the enthusiasm of a man who didn’t particularly care about sticky floors. Lullaby didn't mind. Gallax had given them space to dream. And it had paid off.
</p><p>
The door to the tavern swung open and Rinji stepped inside. Moments later, Dorgle entered, striding with the self-confidence of a man who had recently lost a battle with bureaucracy and decided to fight bureaucracy again.
</p><p>
They spotted Brother Varrus sitting near the hearth, drink in hand, surrounded by a small orbit of locals—traders and weavers and the kind of people who measured time in harvests, not hours.
</p><p>
Dorgle wasted no time. “Brother,” he said, “you’re literate, yes?”
</p><p>
Brother Varrus blinked. “I... am?”
</p><p>
“Great. I need your help with a form.”
</p><p>
And so it began. Together they approached a side table, Varrus gently coaching Dorgle through the enchanted form—the ink shifting subtly as questions were answered, morphing to the user’s intent. But then came the CAPTCHA again.
</p>
---
<p>
Upstairs, Achroteth descended like a whisper of smoke. He moved with that quiet, almost hesitant grace that only druids seem to learn—like they were always afraid to disturb something ancient just out of sight.
</p><p>
He headed for the food, unnoticed by most. But not by Rinji. Rinji approached with a purpose, murmuring something soft. Achroteth nodded and followed, out into the secluded spot behind The Troll's Tollbooth. There, Rinji spoke.
</p><p>
The truth came slowly—like water seeping through cracked stone. He had powers now. Magic. He could cast spells. And he didn’t know why. That’s when Vraxxis appeared again.
</p><p>
Achroteth’s jaw tightened.
</p><p>
“Who are you talking to?”
</p><p>
The wind seemed to pause. The forest held its breath.
</p><p>
Rinji didn’t have an answer. Not one that made sense. Not one that would satisfy the growing tension behind his brother’s eyes.
</p>

## Summary

Rinji snuck away into the forrest just on the outskirts of Petalwood and found a spot that was just perfect. Thick bushes created a wall that could not be penetrated by the prying eyes of anyone passing by. The tall trees and thick foliage created a seive that would dampen the sound of his activities.

With a slight smile he tapped into his new arcane powers, moving piles of dirt with ease, cracking lightning like a whip that protruded from his hands. Rinji did not know what to make of this, but Vraxxis did. And he did not hesitate to administer his own style of motivation, insulting Rinji to try and push him to keep practicing.

The heated exchange led to Vraxxis turning to walk away, with Rinji giving chase and trying to tackle him to the ground but Rinji's attempt to restrain his new "friend" was unsuccessful. 

Dusting himself off, Rinji headed back into town, stridding into a commotion in the town square. At the centre of it of course was Dorgle.

The crowd gathered began to disperse. Fawn and Lullaby headed back to The Troll's Tollbooth. Rinji and Dorgle headed to the blacksmith. Achroteth took a quick excursions to town hall.

As the Druid headed inside, he informed Glendelle of the incoming caravan. Glendelle was filled with panic, overcome with thoughts that she had missed something in the planning of the festival. When Achroteth suggested that perhaps Fawn had organised more chairs and tables, Glendelle's anxiety grew and requested that Achroteth let Finroth know to expect visitors from Glitterfall.

As Achroteth quietly approached the Mayor’s office, he pressed against the door and overheard the quiet laments of Finroth, "Elara, I miss you. I am doing this for you."

Knocking on the door to announce his entry, Achroteth spots Finroth turn around in his chair with a picture frame in hand. Achroteth tells Finroth about the incoming carriage, who is grateful yet confounded about the unexpected visit.

Achroteth turned to leave, closing the door behind him. He found a hidden spot to Wild Shape into a spider and snuck back into the mayor’s office.  As he nestled into a spot high on the bookshelf he witnessed a husband almost overcome with grief for his wife, Finroth staring at the picture on his desk and caressing the pendant hanging from his neck.

Finroth took a moment to compose himself and stood up from his desk. He turned around to face the large window behind his desk and began to weave magic that moved small piles of earth, but something went wrong. Finroth was hit with a backlash of magic that knocked him back.

He quickly returned to his desk, openned a secret drawer and pulled out a book of some kind. He studied over the contents, Achroteth knew immediately, even without being able to read the words on the pages, that this was a spellbook.

When Finroth left, Achroteth took the opportunity to retreat back to The Troll’s Tollbooth and sketch down the symbols and diagrams he saw in Finroth's secret book.

---

At True As Copper, Lerendrie was excited to see Dorgle and Rinji enter his store. He eagerly asked about the progress the visitors had made with securing his permit to sell gems from the Quicklings.

Dorgle bluffed his way through the conversation, convincing the desperate blacksmith that it was as good as done and that the paperwork was just being finalised. 

Dorgle and Rinji sponsored some work, modifications to weapons and armor for the pair. As Lerendrie named his price, Rinji winced in anticipation of Dorgle's disregard for the rules. Surprise followed when Dorgle bartered with the craftsman.

They left the store after paying the agreed price and split up. Rinji went back to the tavern while Dorgle headed to the post Office.

He asked the E-mail Server if there were any responses to the Enchanted Mail he sent to everyone. The E-mail Server informed Dorgle that his message was flagged as SPAM, Sorcerous Pestering by Arcane Messages, and now his account was closed.

Undeterred, Dorgle sought about opening a new account but was met with an obstacle, a form needed to be filled out yet Dorgle could not read. The hesitation flagged concerns with the E-mail Server who presented Dorgle with a CAPTCHA, Completely Arbitrary Pointless Trial of Curious Humanoid Aptitude.

Dorgle took the form and left for the tavern.

---

Lullaby was hard at work with Gallax, pride booming across their face at the knowledge that everyone in the tavern was enjoying fruity cocktails because of their ingenuity.

Rinji entered The Troll's Tollbooth, followed soon after by Dorgle. They spotted Brother Varrus sitting at a table in the crowded common room, enjoying the conversation with local Petalwoodians. Dorgle employed the help of Brother Varrus to complete the new E-mail account form, encountering another CAPTCHA as he tried.

Rinji stole some moments from Achroteth who had come downstairs for some food, taking his brother outside. As Rinji shared the secret of his new found ability to cast spells, Vraxxis appeared again. Achroteth had questions, where did Rinji get his powers, and why was he talking to himself?

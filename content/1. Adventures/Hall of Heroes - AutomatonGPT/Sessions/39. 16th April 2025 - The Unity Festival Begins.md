## Key Events

**Lumday, 9th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 
<p>
The carriages arrived not with trumpets, but with inevitability. They rolled into Petalwood beneath banners stitched with the sigils of Glitterfall, wheels humming over cobbled roads, arcane lanterns glowing steady against the afternoon haze. Their silver finish gleamed, a reminder of power that had not dulled with time or distance.
</p><p>
Fawn stood waiting. Her back was straight, her expression poised, but her heart beat like war drums against her ribs. To any observer, she might’ve seemed like an emissary awaiting royalty. But in truth, she was a daughter bracing for the weight of a past she wore like a corset, tight, proud, and impossible to remove without unravelling.
</p><p>
Finroth and the council arrived moments later, robes freshly pressed, eyes sharp. The Mayor gave the order to the automatons, who began unpacking tables and chairs with inhuman precision. Every movement smooth. Mechanical. Cold.
</p><p>
Members of the delegation from Glitterfall exchanged uncertain glances. Finroth either didn’t notice or didn’t care. Calarel stepped down first. Formal. Distant. The kind of smile that knew how to endure events like these. Their exchange was polite, practiced. Two nobles acting their parts.
</p><p>
Then came Ness, and the façade cracked. Fawn stepped forward. Ness dropped her satchel. And in a blink, arms were wrapped tight and tears whispered into shoulders. It wasn’t loud. It didn’t need to be. It was love, returned like a song long forgotten and now rediscovered.
</p><p>
But even that moment frayed as Finroth approached, his attention drawn to Ness with curious intensity. He introduced himself, and for a man so used to measured charm, something about the interaction shifted. His words slowed. His posture softened. Something unspoken passed between them.
</p><p>
Fawn noticed.
</p><p>
But she said nothing.
</p><p>
Celly. Her presence hit like spring wind against winter skin, warm and too much, too fast. Fawn smiled before she could stop herself. And Celly, stars above, beamed. Their reunion was quiet. Secretive. Loaded with meaning wrapped in silence. But when Celly reached out, fingers brushing against Fawn’s hand, Fawn pulled away.
</p><p>
“We’re just friends,” she said. Loud enough to be overheard. Too loud.
</p><p>
Celly blinked. Her smile faltered. Not disappeared, just… faded. Like something slipping underwater.
</p><p>
Back at the Troll’s Tollbooth, the mood was lighter.
</p><p>
Rinji played his new drum like he’d been born with it stitched to his soul. Rhythmic and sharp, a pulse that matched the heartbeat of the town’s festival spirit.
</p><p>
Achroteth sat quietly, sipping thoughtfully on a Lullatail, letting the flavours settle like magic on his tongue. His eyes, as always, watched the room with more focus than they let on.
</p><p>
Then Fawn arrived, Celly at her side, and all stillness broke.
</p><p>
“This is Celly,” Fawn said.
</p><p>
Lullaby’s eyes widened like moons. “Your wife? Fiancé?”
</p><p>
“No,” Fawn said, a little too fast.
</p><p>
But Lullaby did not let this idea go.
</p><p>
Elsewhere, Finroth and Ness shared a table and a bottle, shadows creeping long as the night stretched on. Their conversation bent gently into intimacy. Their laughter was soft, and sometimes Ness would touch her hair without meaning to. Finroth watched her like she was the only real thing in the room.
</p><p>
Later, Celly and Fawn ascended the stairs together.
</p><p>
Up in Fawn’s room, moonlight painted everything silver. As Fawn and Celly conversed, Fawn admitted that she’d lost the memory of a kiss that Celly described in great detail, of the kiss that sent their relationship on it's journey.
</p>
They spent the night together. No more hiding. No more “just friends.”

**Galeday, 10th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 
<p>
Morning broke pale and quiet.
</p><p>
Achroteth stood by the window, drawing shapes into the sky with magic and mist. His fingers traced glyphs only he could truly understand, crafting a message that would hang above the rooftops like a soft shout:
</p><p>
"PETALWOOD UNITY GALA"
</p><p>
Below, the world began to stir.
</p><p>
Cedrick was the first to notice Achroteth’s pacing. When asked, the druid produced the sketches he had taken from Finroth’s hidden spellbook. Cedrick frowned. Mass Suggestion. Mass Charm Person.
</p><p>
These were not harmless spells.
</p><p>
Fawn found Ness asleep where she had sat, slumped at the same table she'd shared with Finroth.
</p><p>
“Ness?” she asked, softly.
</p><p>
The older woman blinked herself awake, her words slurred. There was a dullness behind her eyes, the tell-tale signs of a potion—or a powder.
</p><p>
Fawn’s breath caught. “You were drugged.”
</p><p>
Ness waved it off. “Just tired, love. Slept in. So much to do.”
</p>
Fawn didn’t argue. Not yet. But she saw the way Ness’s hands shook as she reached for her list.

---
<p>
The Unity Gala opened with applause. Magic shimmered from the stage, illuminating faces both noble and common. The delegation stood tall. The council lined up like painted portraits. And at the front stood Fawn, radiant in her gown. A woman between worlds—trying desperately to belong to both.
</p><p>
Offstage, the rest of the party waited.
</p><p>
The speeches began. Praise. Hopes for unity. Empty phrases wrapped in velvet.
</p><p>
Then, the applause began to slow.
</p><p>
A voice, slurred and heavy with wine, called out from the crowd.
</p><p>
“Boooo!”
</p><p>
Silence.
</p><p>
All heads turned.
</p><p>
Finroth’s eyes narrowed.
</p>
And the machines began to move.



The carriages from Glitterfall arrived beneath a bleached sky, the morning light splintering off polished silver trim and embroidered banners that bore the sigil of Fawn’s royal house. She stood waiting for them, a figure carved of duty and regal composure, her posture straight despite the fatigue etched in the corners of her eyes. She had spent days preparing for this moment—this unveiling of unity. And yet, as the dust from the wheels settled around her, there was a tension in her shoulders that no crown could soften.

Finroth emerged with the council, his smile as practiced as any politician’s—sharp at the edges, just a little too toothy. His metal companions followed like silent wolves: automatons, gleaming in the daylight, already unloading tables and chairs with mechanical precision. The delegation from Glitterfall, cloaked in silks and suspicion, watched with uneasy eyes. This was not how festivities were usually prepared. There was too much order. Too little soul.

Fawn’s reunion with Calarel was polite—formal bows, whispered pleasantries. But with Ness, it broke like a dam. Their embrace was tight and trembling, the silence between them louder than the noise of unpacking crates. Tears were shared without shame—reunions should always taste like salt, after all.

It was short-lived.

Finroth approached, his shadow long even under the sun, and introduced himself to Ness. There was something in his gaze—calculating, curious, perhaps even sincere. And she… she looked at him like someone recognizing a melody long forgotten. They smiled at each other like strangers meeting for the second time.

Off to the side, Celly stood at a respectful distance. When her eyes found Fawn’s, they softened, but the space between them remained taut. The affection was there—obvious to anyone watching—but when Celly stepped forward, Fawn turned aside and spoke the words like a blade: “We are just friends.” The words shattered something between them. You could almost hear it crack.

At the Troll’s Tollbooth, the mood was lighter. Rinji pounded his drum with flair and mischief, drawing cheers from the tavern crowd. Achroteth, curled on a bench with a skewer of sizzling Lullatail, looked more at peace than anyone had seen him in days.

When Fawn entered, she wasn’t alone. Celly walked beside her, shoulders squared but lips tight. Their arrival turned heads—especially Lullaby, who launched into a whimsical tirade about Fawn’s “wife-fiancée-person-of-future-union.” No one stopped her. No one could.

Later that evening, Fawn and Celly climbed the stairs together. Celly mentioned the kiss—the one she'd bartered away to the Quicklings. Fawn didn’t answer, not with words. Whatever remained broken between them, they held together for the night. Sometimes love is quieter than silence.

Morning.

A pale wind stirred the branches of Petalwood, and high above the town, skywriting bloomed in slow, drifting curls:
“PETALWOOD UNITY GALA”

A flourish. A declaration. A warning, perhaps.

Downstairs, Cedrick examined the sketches from Finroth’s so-called spellbook—scraps of arcane diagrams, each marked with symbols for control and suggestion. Mass Charm Person. Mass Suggestion. The kind of magic that didn’t just nudge—it reshaped.

Later, the Gala began.

The square shimmered with lanternlight and hopeful music. Dignitaries from the capital stood alongside farmers and tailors, children weaving between their feet. The council took the stage, Fawn at their center. She looked radiant, but tired—like a star burning its last before dawn.

From the wings, the party watched, tension hanging unspoken between them.

The applause rose. It crested. Then slowed.

A voice cut through it, drunk and brazen.

“Booooooo!”

Gasps. Laughter. Silence.

Finroth raised his hand—and without hesitation, the automatons moved, metal limbs clanking, eyes glowing faint blue as they descended toward the heckler.

And just like that, beneath the fireworks and polished speeches, the truth of the Unity Gala began to show its rust.

## Summary

The carriages from Glitterfall arrive. Fawn is there to greet them, standing with pride at the splendor of the might of her royal family. Finroth and the council arrive to greet the delegation.  He has the automatons start to unpack the chairs and tables for the Unity festival, causing concerned looks from the delegation.

Fawn's reunion with Calarel is brief but cordial. Her reunion with Ness is filled with hugs, concerns and a quiet tear of joy as the stand-in mother and daughter are re-united. It is  cut short by Finroth, who introduces himself to Ness and the pair seem to be quite taken by each other.

Fawn and Celly try to hide their affections for each other as they are finally able to set their eyes on each other. Their relationship is strained when Fawn rebukes Celly's advances, publicly stating that they are friends. 

Back at the Troll's Tollbooth, Rinji is performing for the crowd with his new drum. Achroteth is enjoying a delicious Lullatail and just relaxing.

Fawn arrives with a female elf companion. Fawn introduces her as Celly, and Lullaby goes on a rant about her being Fawn’s wife or fiance or person to marriage.

Ness and Finroth are caught enjoying a drink and conversation, lasting long into the night.

Fawn and Celly head upstairs, Celly mentions the kiss that she gave up to the Quicklings, they spend the night together.

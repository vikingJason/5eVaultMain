## Key Events

**Galeday, 10th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 
</p><p>
The sun dipped low over Petalwood, casting long, golden shadows across a town straining to hold its own weight. The Royal Feast loomed near, like a page turned in a book where no one had agreed what came next.
</p><p>
Time slipped by unnoticed, until it was too late to catch.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Lullaby, Rinji, and Dorgle stepped through the rusted archway of the Militia Hall, boots clicking on stone tiled floors stained with time and regulation. The hall was quieter now. That eerie, brittle kind of silence that came only after something irreversible.
</p><p>
The door to the Sheriff’s Office stood untouched, a plaque of aged brass glinting in the light. For a breathless moment, they remembered why they had come to Petalwood in the first place. Elrohir. The missing lawman. The spark that lit the journey.
</p><p>
A Sergeant-at-Arms Automaton greeted them with the indifference only cold programming could achieve. His report came clipped and efficient:
</p><p>
“The prisoner has been processed. Along with others. Executed for inciting unrest against Petalwood.”
</p><p>
He offered no guilt. No grief. Not even the pretence of justification. Just the truth, laid out with all the compassion of a falling guillotine.
</p><p>
Something in Lullaby’s chest tightened, and it never quite unclenched.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Cedrick and Achroteth arrived at Town Hall, now transformed into a palace of celebration. The high chamber swelled with laughter and courtly music. Fey dignitaries sparkled in their ceremonial silks, Petalwood’s council smiled too easily. A perfect image. A polished lie.
</p><p>
Achroteth offered polite words, nods with just the right amount of reverence, but soon excused himself. Althea followed, her expression set in a line too taut to be coincidence.
</p><p>
They met in hushed corners.
</p><p>
“Finroth,” Achroteth said, “he may be using enchantments. You’ll need something to shield your mind if this turns... subtle.”
</p><p>
Althea nodded. No questions. Just the shared understanding of those who’d seen too much to dismiss shadows.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Back at The Troll’s Tollbooth, news moved faster than breath. Lullaby had returned. The report delivered.
</p><p>
The prisoners were gone.
</p><p>
Executed.
</p><p>
Fawn’s face shifted, rage hardening like clay in fire. She stormed off alone, her royal demeanour slipping beneath the weight of fury. Her footsteps echoed like accusations.
</p><p>
The others dressed. Faces painted with composure. Ness and Cellie joined them as they stepped out into streets drenched in colour.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
The Town Square bloomed with joy not quite earned. Streamers. Floral towers. Tables polished and dressed in white linen. Villagers laughed, unaware of the storm unraveling behind their smiles.
</p><p>
Fawn cut through the crowd like a blade of purpose, eyes locked on Mayor Finroth. Their conversation, if it could be called that, was measured and sharp.
</p><p>
He greeted her warmly. Offered apologies. Assured her that perhaps this was all a misunderstanding. A miscommunication.
</p><p>
His smile was too soft. His answers too easy.
</p><p>
When the rest of the party arrived, the tension hung like smoke. They were seated at the main table, faces smiling for the crowd, hands clenched beneath the linen.
</p><p>
Rinji whispered to Vraxxis again, and though none could see the spectral presence beside him, the conversation wasn’t subtle. Finroth’s gaze shifted, sharp and curious.
</p><p>
“Do you see her?” he asked Rinji softly, voice almost kind. “My wife. Elara.”
</p><p>
The table went quiet.
</p><p>
Then, the first course arrived.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**First Course – Red Theme**
**Scarlet Gazpacho with Chorizo Crumbles**
A chilled tomato and red pepper soup, garnished with diced cucumber, croutons, and spicy chorizo crumbles for added depth and flavour. The vibrant red colour symbolizes the rich harvests of Petalwood.
This raw dish also honours the founders' resilience during harsh conditions. 
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Conversation tried to resume.
</p><p>
Achroteth spoke quietly with Ness, tracing her memories back to names like Butch and Cassidy.
</p><p>
Rinji, emboldened, asked Cellie if she would fight the Head Librarian to claim her post.
</p><p>
She did not laugh.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**Palate Cleanser: Orange Theme**
**Mandarin Sorbet with Mint**
Refreshing mandarin sorbet infused with fresh mint leaves, providing a crisp and clean palate cleanser.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Rinji played his drum. The sound bounced off the cobblestones, unwelcome. A flutist returned the favour with wild, raw Fey magic, a surge of chaotic silence that stole Rinji’s voice.
</p><p>
The crowd applauded.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**Second Course: Orange Theme**
**Butternut Squash and Apple Soup with Duck Confit**
A smooth and velvety soup combining roasted butternut squash with sweet apples, topped with tender duck confit and toasted pumpkin seeds.
Accompanied by a piece of bread, broken in half to honour the spirits of those lost to [[The Blight]].
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
As they ate, Dorgle stood and announced that Rinji would now make a speech.
</p><p>
Rinji stood. Opened his mouth. Nothing.
</p><p>
He mimed the words, fury in his hands, until just as he finished, the silence lifted. A cruel trick of timing.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**Third Course: Yellow Theme**
**Saffron Risotto with Grilled Asparagus**
Creamy saffron-infused risotto served with grilled asparagus and a sprinkle of Parmesan cheese.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Dorgle wandered to the Fey guests. It took moments, mere moments, before Automatons descended and arrested him without spectacle.
</p><p>
Fawn’s plans, woven from duty and hope, began to splinter. She could feel them slipping through her fingers.
</p><p>
Rinji followed Dorgle, shadows clinging to him.
</p><p>
Ness and Lullaby argued over Achroteth’s asparagus, their tension breaking the air like static.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**Palate Cleanser: Green Theme**
**Basil Lime Sorbet**
A palate-cleansing sorbet made from fresh basil and lime, offering a cool and invigorating taste..
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**Fourth Course: Green Theme**
**Herb-Crusted Glimmerfin**
A locally sourced Glimmerfin Fish fillet, herb-crusted and baked to perfection, served with a side of sautéed asparagus and a light lemon butter sauce.
Served without red wine to respect the tradition of appropriate pairings.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
At the table, Finroth’s hand rarely left his pendant. He rubbed it absently, constantly, like it anchored him to something unseen.
</p><p>
They noticed.
</p><p>
They said nothing.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Rinji, invisible now, trailed the Automatons. Their movement was... precise. Too precise. Their eyes twitched, ever so slightly, as if following unseen cues.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
**Fifth Course: Blue Theme**
**Seared Scallops with Blueberry Gastrique**
Tender seared scallops drizzled with a tangy blueberry gastrique, served with a side of wild rice.
Guests enjoy the rich and earthy flavors, reflecting the connection to Petalwood’s natural surroundings.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
Under his plate, Achroteth found a note.
</p><p>
He pocketed it. Left.
</p><p>
The Troll’s Tollbooth answered his presence like an old friend. There, he cast his spell.
</p><p>
The note was from Althea.
> “Dorgle is safe. Watched. Do not worry.”
</p><p>
On his way back, a drunk villager attempted to sit beside Finroth.
</p><p>
Finroth’s smile evaporated.
</p><p>
He clenched the pendant, and within seconds, the man was escorted away.
</p>

---

<p>
<strong>Sixth Course: Violet Theme</strong>
**Amethyst Lavender Tart**
A delicate tart filled with a rich lavender-infused custard, topped with fresh blackberries and a sprinkle of edible violet petals, served with a side of dairy-free vanilla bean ice cream.
</p>

---
<p>
As spoons scraped porcelain, a surprise unfolded: every Petalwoodian received an enchanted message. A fabricated raffle invitation, courtesy of Dorgle.
</p><p>
Finroth blinked once.
</p><p>
And smiled.
</p>

---

<p>
By the fountain, Fawn gave Cellie a ring, a promise. A keepsake from her mother. A thread of legacy and love, given freely.
</p><p>
Elsewhere, Finroth requested a private word with Ness.
</p><p>
Achroteth followed, unseen, a spider now, watching from shadows.
</p><p>
They talked.
</p><p>
He spoke of Elara. Of grief.
</p><p>
She spoke of her past.
</p><p>
Then, like a blade with no handle, he ended it.
</p><p>
“This is going nowhere.”
</p><p>
She slapped him. Ran.
</p><p>
Achroteth shifted form, rushed to find the others. When they did, Ness’s eyes were rimmed red. Tears and something more.
</p><p>
He cast Detect Magic.
</p><p>
Nothing.
</p><p>
Only heartbreak.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
The hum of magical servos died all at once.
</p><p>
Lullaby froze mid-step, the echo of their boots swallowed by a sudden, unnatural stillness. The Automatons that lined the Militia Hall, a wall of polished brass and unfailing obedience, stood frozen in place. No warning, no command. Just... off.
</p><p>
Rinji glanced around warily, already slipping behind Cedrick with the subtle precision of someone used to surviving on instinct alone. "That’s... not ominous at all," he muttered.
</p><p>
Dorgle emerged from a holding cell moments later, the door swinging open without sound. No chains, no stern speech, not even the bureaucratic sneer of dismissal from the Sergeant-at-Arms. Just silence. Release without reason.
</p><p>
"You're free?" Cedrick asked. It wasn’t a question, not really.
</p><p>
"Aye," Dorgle replied, brushing dust from his vest. “Guess they got bored of me.”
</p><p>
"Or someone pulled strings," Lullaby said, their voice distant. Calculating. They were already moving toward the Sheriff’s Office.
</p><p>
They didn’t question it. Not out loud.
</p><p>
The office was chaos incarnate, furniture overturned, shelves stripped bare, even the modest flag of Petalwood torn from its mount. The room felt violated, like a mind invaded and memories torn free.
</p><p>
“Someone was searching for something,” Cedrick whispered, kneeling by the overturned desk.
</p><p>
“Or trying to make it look like they were,” Rinji added, always one step ahead of paranoia.
</p><p>
Beneath the desk, beneath the damage, beyond the rage, Cedrick found it. A hidden compartment, untouched by the mess. Subtle, clever craftsmanship. Within: a map of the town marked in fine ink, a collection of sealed letters, a ledger full of coded entries...and a ring.
</p><p>
It was gold, but unadorned save for a single sigil, an eclipse, half-sun half-shadow. Cedrick felt it before he understood it. Memory stirred. Flickers of firelight illuminating cloaked men in his father’s study. Quiet voices, urgent and low. A phrase spoken not in fear, but in reverence.
</p><p>
The Dawn’s Shadow.
</p><p>
Cedrick's breath caught. He turned the ring in his fingers, the weight of it pressing decades into his palm. His father had said little, but what he did share etched itself into Cedrick’s bones:
</p><p>
“The Dawn’s Shadow exists not to win wars, but to end them before they begin. We are the blade in the scabbard of peace.”
</p><p>
An organisation buried within the ranks of the Knights of the First Order. Operatives trained in subtlety, manipulation, and strategic mercy. Peace at any cost.
</p><p>
They infiltrated potential threats, eliminated dangerous agents before violence could spread, and most of all, they stayed invisible.
</p><p>
Cedrick slid the ring into his cloak and gathered the remaining documents. Already, the hum of reactivation stirred in the hall behind them.
</p><p>
“Time’s up,” he said.
</p><p>
They left before the Automatons fully stirred again.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
The Troll’s Tollbooth was alive with tension, the air thick with unsaid truths. Dorgle stomped toward the bar for a drink, muttering about malfunctioning guard dogs and the hazards of incarceration. Rinji peeled away into a shadowed corner, ears twitching. Cedrick held the ring in his pocket like it might burn him.
</p><p>
Fawn paced. She hadn’t spoken yet, not really. Until, “Calarel wasn’t at the feast.”
</p><p>
The words hit like a thrown stone in a still pond.
</p><p>
The group froze, thoughts already racing toward implications. But before they could unpack the significance, the door creaked open and Althea stepped in, quiet, sharp-eyed, and clearly expecting confrontation.
</p><p>
She got it.
</p><p>
Fawn spun on her, voice tight. “Where were you during the feast?”
</p><p>
Achroteth pushed forward next. “Your mayor’s arresting  folks without cause. Executing others. The shiny festival’s got a rotting core.”
</p><p>
Cedrick tossed the map onto the table, followed by the ledger. “This was hidden in the Sheriff’s office. Someone was looking for it. Someone with enough power to silence dissent... permanently.”
</p><p>
Althea stood tall through it all, but her eyes tightened at the edges. She scanned the room, taking in more than words, reading body language, weighing intent.
</p><p>
And then... she spoke. Carefully.
</p><p>
“What you’ve found is dangerous.”
</p><p>
Silence followed. It wasn’t a denial.
</p><p>
Outside, the laughter of festival-goers filtered in through the wooden shutters. Music played, wine poured, lights danced across flower-strewn tables. A celebration.
</p><p>
Inside, trust was fraying. Secrets were surfacing.
</p><p>
And someone had decided the masquerade was over.
</p><p></p>

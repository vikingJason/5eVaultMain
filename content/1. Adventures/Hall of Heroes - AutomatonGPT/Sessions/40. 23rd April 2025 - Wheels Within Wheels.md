## Key Events

**Galeday, 10th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 
<p>
The sky over Petalwood stretched pale and cloudless, like it was waiting, watching, for something to break the illusion of peace.
</p><p>
The Royal Feast was still hours away, but tension hung in the air like incense. Sharp. Sweet. Suffocating.
</p><p>
The group had gathered, as they often did, in the quiet just before something important. No grand strategy session, no battle plan, just a quiet understanding that something was not as it should be.
</p><p>
Lullaby peeled away from the group, trailing the drunk man the automatons had dragged from the Gala. The militia hall was a repurposed building, dressed in urgency and rust. It stank of sweat and static.
</p><p>
Inside, they found the prisoner held in a holding cell, temporary, but thick with silence. A Sergeant-class Automaton, boxy and bureaucratic, greeted them with the emotionless cadence of something that had never been kissed, never been loved, never been truly alive.
</p><p>
Permission was granted.
</p><p>
The prisoner wasn’t what they expected. His anger was not reckless, it was weary. His words slurred, but pointed.
</p><p>
“They eat well,” he muttered. “Up there, in the council hall. While the children in the southern quarter suck on hollow roots and dream of broth.”
</p><p>
He did not hate the Fey. He hated the rot behind polished smiles.
</p><p>
Unity, he said, meant little when your stomach growled louder than the speeches.
</p>
---
<p>
Outside, Fawn moved through the square with the kind of urgency that could cut through stone. Her gaze fell upon Glendelle, the mayoral aide, armed with a clipboard and fury. She was directing automatons like a conductor trying to wrangle a symphony of gears and servos.
</p><p>
“I need the agenda,” Fawn asked, her voice poised.
</p><p>
The request nearly broke Glendelle.
</p><p>
The woman blinked once, twice, and then launched into a spiral of muttering stress, papers fluttering, ink splashing, orders barked with desperation. She had crafted the event like a glass sculpture, and now someone wanted to revise it the day of.
</p><p>
That’s when Achroteth stepped in.
</p><p>
“Breathe,” he said gently. “Let me help.”
</p><p>
He whispered the words of Longstrider, weaving speed into Glendelle’s stride. Then Lesser Restoration, soothing the edge from her panic like wind through grass.
</p><p>
Magic did not always need to be grand to matter.
</p><p>
Cedrick examined the automatons, running his fingers over the steelwork with a craftsman’s curiosity. These weren’t mass-produced models from the coastal workshops. They were custom, newer designs, leaner joints, reinforced hulls. Guard units.
</p><p>
Not for farming.
</p><p>
Not for hosting festivals.
</p><p>
They were built to enforce.
</p><p>
Achroteth, ever the quiet storm, made his way to the Post Office. Not for news. But to send it.
</p><p>
The E-mail Server, accepted his request to deliver enchanted correspondence. Arcane ink shimmered faintly as the druid began to write.
</p>
> To Varenthis,
> 
> It has been quite some time since I left the mountain. I did not realise that the enchanted mail would be able to reach you in the circle but I am pleased to be able to update you on my journey. I have travelled all the way to the capital and before I could inspect their grand library, I was accosted by the chief librarian and sent on a mission to escort the princess to a small town of Petalwood. Upon arrival, the place felt off. There were machines farming the fields and all over the town. I was able to resolve an issue with a group of those machines attempting to deforest a massive section of the forest, helping a dryad [[Laurelia]] save her home from the deforestation and disruption of the cycle.
> 
> Petalwood is hosting a unification festival and it seeks to bring together the humans and the fey, to me this seems overly grandiose for something so simple. I am hoping that it will be a success and my travels will continue.
> 
> I am curious about your and our circle's relation with Sehanine Moonbow as one of the people I am travelling with is a follower of hers.
> 
> Be well, and may the Circle be unbroken
<p>
He sealed the letter with the sap of the Silverleaf Tree, then sent it into the arcane system.
</p><p>
In silence, he drafted one more letter, this one to Elrohir, the elusive sheriff whose absence whispered like a missing beat in a song.
</p><p>
Then, for the first time in a long while, Cedrick wrote a letter of his own, this one not to a council or a Circle, but to his mother. The kind of letter that carries more than information.
</p><p>
A portal appeared, out stepped a clerk dressed not in robes or armor, but a grey buttoned shirt and satchel, horns protruding from his head. The Mailer Daemon.
</p><p>
With a cough and the kind of bureaucratic sympathy only a postal worker could muster, he delivered the news.
</p><p>
“Your mail to Elrohir,” he said. “Bounced.”
</p><p>
Magic had rules. Even in a world of wonders.
</p><p>
Achroteth handed it to Cedrick.
</p><p>
“Read it,” he said. “But not here.”
</p><p>
Cedrick tucked the message away, eyes shadowed with thought.
</p>
---
<p>
Lullaby returned to the group, summarizing the prisoner’s rant with her characteristic clarity.
</p><p>
“It’s not the Fey,” she said. “It’s the imbalance. The inequality.”
</p>



## Summary

**Galeday, 10th Of Emberleaf, Cycle 6880 CCE** 

With a few hours before the Royal Feast was due to begin, the party gathered to work out what they would do. 

Lullaby follows the drunk guy being dragged to a makeshift militia hall.

Inside, they talk with the Sergeant bot, getting permission to talk to the prisoner. They spot an office, marked for the Sheriff, remembering that they were tasked to find Elrohir.

Lullaby talks with the prisoner, learning that he was unhappy and unconvinced by the Unity Festival, while many of the Petalwoodians starve at the hand of the decision of the council.

---

Spotting Glendelle with clipboard in hand, barking orders at Automatons, Fawn headed over to the busy mayoral aide, requesting some help from her to get the agenda for the festival. This sent Glendelle into a tailspin, already stressing over all the final meticulous details to ensure the perfect event.

Achroteth found a moment to pull Glendelle aside, informing her that he can use Locate Objects and Skywrite if they need it and cast both Longstrider and Lesser Restoration on them.

Cedrick inspects the automatons, seeing that they were newer looking models and found there are custom made guard bots.

Achroteth headed to the post office and asked if they can send an email to the Archdruid Varenthis of the mountain. They can so he left, heading back to the Troll's Tollbooth to put together a letter.

---

Lullaby tells the group that the drunk man is upset at the town for letting people starve and the inequality of the town (not the fey).

Achroteth takes some time to write the letter.

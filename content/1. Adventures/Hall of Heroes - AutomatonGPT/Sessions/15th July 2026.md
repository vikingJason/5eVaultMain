Have Cedrick stay because **everyone _except_ the Collector** convinces him.

# The Real Ending

The Prince loses.

Not in combat.

Not politically.

He loses because, for the first time, everyone around him stops enabling him.

That is a much more satisfying ending.

---

# Act I – The Truth Comes Out

After the battle, the toyshop is a mess.

Stuffing everywhere.

Broken toys.

The Prince is sitting in the middle of it all, sulking.

The Elder Goliath (I'd love to help name him later) quietly begins cleaning up without a word.

Then Starfox and several Whisperers arrive.

They've tracked the Ashen Cult to the lair.

After searching the toyshop they uncover...

## The Unicorn Vault

Hidden beneath the workshop is a cold storage room.

Inside are neatly catalogued unicorn horns.

Hundreds of them.

Each labelled.

Some still have dried blood at the base.

The room goes silent.

Even the Prince looks confused why everyone is upset.

> "They're very pretty."

> "I was saving them."

To him they're collectibles.

To everyone else...

They're evidence.

---

# Act II – The Confrontation

Starfox wants to arrest the Prince.

The Goliath steps between them.

Not to protect the Prince...

But because arresting him would be a disaster.

He is tied to the crystal network.

Nobody knows what killing or imprisoning him would do.

Instead the Goliath finally says something he has probably wanted to say for centuries:

> "No."

The Prince freezes.

> "...what?"

> "No."

The babysitter has never refused him.

Ever.

---

# Act III – Consequences

The Prince has a tantrum.

Not combat.

An emotional collapse.

Lightning.

Toy shelves flying apart.

Storm clouds gathering.

The Goliath simply walks over...

...picks him up under one arm like an unruly child.

The Prince kicks and screams.

The Goliath doesn't react.

This immediately tells the players everything they need to know.

For centuries...

He could have stopped this at any time.

He simply believed he shouldn't.

---

# Act IV – Cedrick's Opportunity

Now the Goliath turns to Cedrick.

Not because he's an Artificer.

Because he's watched him.

> "You build."

> "You repair."

> "You improve."

He gestures around the toyshop.

> "Imagine if this place made joy..."

"...instead of hoarding it."

Then he reveals his dream.

---

# The Dream

The palace becomes:

## Neverenough Toys

A magical workshop.

Children from across Calindor receive toys.

Not because they're rich.

Because every child deserves one.

The crystal-powered machinery could produce:

- prosthetics
- enchanted educational toys
- mobility aids
- comforting plush companions
- practical inventions

Instead of feeding one spoiled Prince...

The workshop serves the whole continent.

---

# Why Cedrick?

The Goliath says:

> "I can swing a hammer."

> "I cannot build a future."

Cedrick can.

---

# The Prince's Reaction

Naturally...

He hates the idea.

> "No!"

> "They're MY toys!"

> "He's MY builder!"

Eventually...

He points at Cedrick.

> "...Fine."

"...but he has to visit."

Then...

> "...every week."

The Goliath:

> "We'll negotiate."

---

# The Whisperers

Starfox isn't thrilled.

The Prince has committed terrible acts.

But...

Executing or imprisoning him is too dangerous.

Instead they strike an agreement.

The Whisperers monitor the toyshop.

The Goliath takes over daily operations.

Cedrick modernises everything.

The Prince...

...has to learn to share.

Whether he likes it or not.

---

# Cedrick's Farewell

As the party prepares to leave...

The Prince runs over.

Holding something behind his back.

He awkwardly thrusts it toward Cedrick.

It's...

A badly stitched plush doll.

Clearly made by the Prince himself.

It looks terrible.

One eye is too high.

The stitching is crooked.

The boots are different sizes.

> "I..."

Long pause.

> "...made you."

Not:

> "...made this for you."

Made _you._

Then, looking away:

> "...so you don't forget."

That one line tells the players everything.

This immortal child who spent centuries collecting things...

...finally tried creating something instead.

It's awful.

It's heartfelt.

It's growth.

---

# Epilogue

Months later, rumours begin spreading across Calindor.

Children are receiving extraordinary toys with a tiny stitched tag that reads:

> **"Made at Neverenough Workshop."**

Every toy has two signatures.

One is a neat craftsman's mark.

**Cedrick.**

The other is written in glittery crayon.

**PROPERTY OF THE PRINCE!**

The Goliath has clearly compromised: the Prince gets to feel important, every child gets a toy, and Cedrick leaves behind a legacy that feels funny, hopeful, and uniquely his. It also leaves the door open for the Prince to reappear later—perhaps a little wiser, perhaps still delightfully impossible.

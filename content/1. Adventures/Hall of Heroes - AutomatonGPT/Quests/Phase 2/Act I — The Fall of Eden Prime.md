I think this is the perfect opportunity to make **Eden Prime feel like Jurassic Park after the power has gone out**. The players shouldn't think, "We're arriving at a city." They should think, "We've arrived at the greatest magical nature reserve in Calindor... and something has gone terribly wrong."

I also wouldn't reveal the Heartstone corruption immediately. Let the players believe this is a wildlife disaster before slowly realizing it is much larger.

---

# Act I — The Fall of Eden Prime

_Levels 7–8_

## Campaign Goals

By the end of Act I, the party should:

- Fall in love with Eden Prime.
    
- Meet the major NPCs.
    
- Witness the effects of Heartstone corruption firsthand.
    
- Learn of the Blightfather and the Ashen Cult.
    
- Discover the existence of Vael'Vyren.
    
- Be tasked with recovering the Observatory Map.
    
- Relocate the expedition to Vael'Vyren.

# Opening Scene

## "Something is Hunting."

As the party travels toward Eden Prime, the forest feels...wrong.

The birds have stopped singing.

Crystalline flowers have blackened.

The wind carries distant roars.

Then...

A flare streaks into the sky.

Moments later...

A bloodied ranger bursts from the trees.

> "RUN!"

Behind them crashes one of the reserve's guardians.

## First Encounter

### Corrupted Fey Dinosaur

Rather than simply a dinosaur...

Imagine a creature touched by the Fey.

Perhaps:

**Thorncrest Stalker**

- Crystal antlers
    
- Flowering vines across its back

The corruption causes:

- Crystal tumors
    
- Eyes leaking black smoke

It is simultaneously majestic and horrifying.

# Midpoint Disaster

After recovering enough information...

Everything changes.

The Heartstone beneath Eden Prime fractures.

Not an explosion.

Reality starts...

slipping.

Examples:

- Trees grow upside down.
    
- Gravity reverses briefly.
    
- Dinosaurs vanish and reappear.
    
- Time repeats in isolated pockets.
    
- Flowers bloom from stone.
    
- Fey music echoes from nowhere.
    

The city cannot remain inhabited.

# Ending Scene

The expedition reaches an immense stone doorway hidden beneath an overgrown cliff, inscribed with elegant Tyrellion script and surrounded by crystal formations that pulse faintly in time with the Heartstones. The entrance is sealed, untouched for millennia.

Lullaby steps forward almost instinctively.

As their hand touches the central glyph, the dormant mechanism stirs.

Crystal veins blaze to life beneath the stone.

The great doors part with a deep, resonant hum.

Warm light spills into the darkness beyond.

A calm, melodic voice echoes through the chamber—not loud, but impossibly clear.

> **"Tyrellion authorization detected."**

A pause.

> **"Welcome... home."**

No one speaks.

The scholars stand in awe.

The rangers grip their weapons a little tighter.

Even the Whispering Court agent lowers their head in quiet reverence.

For the first time in thousands of years, **Vael'Vyren has accepted a living heir of the Tyrellion.**

## One idea I think would make Eden Prime unforgettable

Instead of a traditional zoo or reserve, make the entire region a **living restoration project**. The Tyrellion once cultivated and protected Calindor's most extraordinary creatures here, and modern scholars have continued that mission. The "dinosaurs" aren't simply prehistoric beasts—they're descendants of ancient Fey megafauna that coexist with unicorns, blink elk, crystal tortoises, pollen drakes, and other magical wildlife.

That way, when corruption spreads, the tragedy isn't just that monsters attack. It's that a place devoted to harmony between civilization and nature is unraveling before the players' eyes. Saving Eden Prime becomes emotionally meaningful, even if they ultimately have to abandon it and make Vael'Vyren their new sanctuary.

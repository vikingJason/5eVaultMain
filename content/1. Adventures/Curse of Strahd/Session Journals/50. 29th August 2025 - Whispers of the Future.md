## Summary

The air in the Amber Temple always seemed to watch.
</p><p>
Dust floated in lazy swirls through the stillness, glittering in the faint light of Marcus’s spell. The chamber felt more mausoleum than room, amber sarcophagi standing like patient sentinels, their smooth surfaces humming with a dark promise.
</p><p>
Marcus stood in their midst, one hand pressed to his cheek. The new eye blinked.
</p><p>
Aeli’s grip on her sword tightened as if expecting it to lunge from Marcus’s face and bite someone. “You’re… fine?”
</p><p>
“Fine is a word,” Marcus said, lowering his hand. “I am… still me.”
</p><p>
Lars leaned closer, his usual pragmatism shading into morbid fascination. “That thing is moving,” he said flatly. “And it’s not moving when you blink.”
</p><p>
“Future Marcus will deal with it.” He said it lightly. Aeli didn’t look reassured.
</p><p>
“Of course,” Athun murmured, dry as the dust around them.
</p><p>
And with that, Marcus touched the next sarcophagus.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
The world fell away.
</p><p>
Marcus stood in a cave of mist and shadow, its walls dripping with unseen water. Something moved in the dark, robes first, then pale hands, and a face with no eyes, no mouth, only a faint hollow glow where features should have been.
</p><p>
“I am bound,” it whispered, voice like wind through a graveyard. “But I offer life. Power to drain it, power to command it. Free me.”
</p><p>
Marcus’s heart thudded in his chest, and he grinned with too many teeth, though the grin faltered as one came loose and clattered to the stone floor.
</p><p>
“Yes,” he said.
</p><p>
The cave vanished. Marcus staggered back into himself, the temple rushing into view. Teeth fell from his mouth, pattering on the stone like hailstones. He spat one out, wiped his chin, and shrugged.
</p><p>
“Adventure,” he mumbled, lips already shaping into something closer to a grimace than a smile.
</p><p>
Athun tilted his head. “Nothing bad ever happens in threes.”
</p><p>
Marcus’s hand was already reaching for the final sarcophagus.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
This vision was different.
</p><p>
Moonlight spilled across cobblestones as Marcus found himself in a silent city, the wind carrying the scent of cold marble. A regal man stepped from the shadows, half his face and one arm sculpted from amber shot through with veins of light.
</p><p>
“I am Xantress,” the man said. His voice was a king’s voice, the kind that could silence a room with a single word. “Your presence is… forgettable. I will make you impossible to ignore.”
</p><p>
He held out his amber hand.
</p><p>
Marcus smiled, what remained of it, and took the bargain.
</p><p>
When he returned, he felt… more. His shadow seemed to stand straighter, his very presence demanding attention. The final sarcophagus cracked, spewing smoke into the chamber.
</p><p>
Lars backed away a step. “You feel… louder.”
</p><p>
“Good,” Marcus said, and smiled again.
</p><p>
The hall was waiting for them, as if it knew they would come back.
</p><p>
Green light flickered from the alcove, Flameskulls, their empty sockets burning like watchfires.
</p><p>
“They’re back,” Aeli said, raising her sword.
</p><p>
“They were never gone,” Lars muttered.
</p><p>
The first skull rose into view and spat fire, searing Lars and nearly felling him where he stood. Another darted down the hall.
</p><p>
Marcus snapped a word and threw a dome of ice across the corridor, encasing one skull in frozen blue. It shattered its prison seconds later, shrieking in anger.
</p><p>
Aeli hurled holy water, and missed entirely, splashing Athun’s boots.
</p><p>
“Now my shoe is consecrated,” Athun said without expression.
</p><p>
“Shut up,” Aeli growled, already uncorking another vial.
</p><p>
The tide turned in blood and light. Lars crushed one skull with his mace, Aeli’s sun blade cleaved another, and Marcus at last connected with his holy water, the skull sizzling to nothing.
</p><p>
They salted the stones with holy water, just to be sure. Marcus swore he saw a third skull watching from the darkness, but when they advanced, the hall was empty.
</p><p>
The double doors creaked open to reveal fur bedrolls, a ragged fissure letting in the mountain wind, and six barbarian berserkers, caught in the middle of preparing for something.
</p><p>
Their leader roared, spears already in hand. The direwolf at his side growled, low and deep.
</p><p>
The fight was chaos from the first heartbeat. Berserkers charged the hall, some forcing their way around to flank, their leader staying behind to hurl javelins with uncanny accuracy.
</p><p>
Marcus slammed the staff of frost to the floor, ice sweeping into a towering wall that split the room, blocking half the attackers and pelting the rest with frozen shards.
</p><p>
“Nice,” Lars grunted, cutting one down with a swing of his axe.
</p><p>
Aeli darted behind another, her blade punching through its spine.
</p><p>
Athun grew. Literally, his form swelling into a giant silhouette that filled the corridor, halberd striking through the doorway like the arm of a siege engine.
</p><p>
The wall of ice shattered as the berserker chief’s axe slammed into Marcus, dropping the wizard to one knee and breaking his concentration.
</p><p>
Marcus looked up, blood running from his mouth. Then he smiled that gap-toothed grin, raised a hand, and called down an Ice Storm that turned the room into a howling blizzard.
</p><p>
When the hail stopped falling, only one berserker stood. Aeli cut him down.
</p><p>
The direwolf whined once, then bolted into the fissure and out into the wilds.
</p><p>
The room stank of blood and iron. They stripped the fallen of their weapons, the weight of greataxes heavy in their hands. Somewhere in the mountains, the wolf howled its grief, long and lonely.
</p><p>
Lars wiped his blade clean. “They were living here. Guardians. Worshippers. Something.”
</p><p>
Athun said nothing.
</p><p>
Marcus stood amidst the corpses, the new eye on his cheek unblinking.
</p><p>
The Amber Temple had begun to name its price.
</p><p>
And it was far from done with them.

## Summary - Draft

The air within the Amber Temple was cold and still, thick with the dust of ages and the weight of forbidden power. Our party—the ever-curious wizard Marcus, the stalwart fighter Aeli, the pragmatic ranger Lars, and the contemplative Athun—stood amidst a circle of amber sarcophagi, each a prison for a dark vestige of power. The consequences of curiosity were already etched upon Marcus's face, quite literally, in the form of a new, unblinking eye set in the middle of his cheek that seemed to observe the world with a sentience not his own.

The party’s attention was consumed by Marcus’s new… feature. Aeli shielded herself in anticipation of further corruption, while Lars studied the eye with a wary fascination, noting its unsettling, independent movement. “I feel like something's watching us out of your face and it's not you,” he remarked, a statement that did little to comfort anyone. With a shrug and the immortal words of adventurers everywhere—“Future Marcus’s problem”—the wizard turned his attention to the next sarcophagus.

Upon touching the amber, Marcus’s consciousness was pulled into a bleak, fog-shrouded cave where a dark-robed figure awaited. The entity, introducing itself as a prisoner of Vampyr, offered a grim gift: the Gift of Life. In exchange for freedom, it promised Marcus power over the lifeforce of others. Eager for any advantage against the vampire lord Strahd, Marcus agreed. He awoke back in the temple to the sound of stones clattering to the floor—the sound of his own teeth falling out. His smile was now a vacant, grimacing hole. “It's fine. It's all part of the adventure,” he mumbled, undeterred. When Athun dryly noted, “Nothing bad happens in threes,” Marcus was already reaching for the third coffin.

This time, his spirit was transported to a deserted, moonlit city where a regal gentleman with an arm and half his face sculpted from transparent, arcane-swirled amber greeted him. This was Xantress, the Kingmaker. He spoke of reputation and presence, offering Marcus a potent boost to his charisma in exchange for liberation. They sealed the pact with a handshake that crackled with magical energy. Returning to his body, Marcus opened the final sarcophagus. A gust of wind and black smoke poured forth, and the party watched as Marcus’s form seemed to solidify, his presence becoming undeniably more compelling. He had gained a formidable boost to his Charisma.

Leaving the sarcophagi chamber, the party ascended the stairs, past the shattered remains of the amber golem they had previously bested, and back into the temple’s main halls. A sinister green glow emanating from the hole in the alcove where they had first encountered a flameskull signaled that their rest was over. More of the undead guardians had arrived.

The battle was swift but punishing. A flameskull emerged, its eye rays searing Lars and nearly felling the ranger in a single, brutal strike. A second skull appeared, darting down the corridor. Marcus reacted with potent magic, attempting to encase one skull in a domed **Wall of Ice**. The skull retaliated with fire, quickly shattering its frozen prison. The party’s initial attempts to use holy water were comically disastrous, with both Aeli and Marcus accidentally splashing Athun’s boots instead of their targets. “Now his shoe is nice and clean,” the DM noted wryly.

The tide turned with solid steel and radiant light. Lars, revived by Aeli’s healing magic, crushed one skull with his mace. Aeli, finally drawing her sun blade, carved into another with righteous fury. Marcus finally connected with a vial of holy water, extinguishing the final skull in a sizzle of sanctified power. As they sprinkled holy water on the remains to prevent regeneration, Marcus swore he’d seen a third skull, but a cautious, stealthy advance down the hall revealed nothing but empty, echoing darkness.

Pressing on, the party came to a set of double doors. Marcus threw them open to reveal a grim barracks: six fur bedrolls, a fissure in the wall letting in the mountain cold, and a group of bloodthirsty barbarian berserkers and their massive direwolf, all caught in a moment of preparation.

The fight was a chaotic melee. The berserkers surged forward with brute force, some rushing into the hallway to flank the party while their leader hung back, hurling spears with deadly accuracy. The direwolf added to the chaos with a terrifying howl that echoed through the chambers. Marcus, again employing the **Staff of Frost**, summoned a massive **Wall of Ice** to block the main doorway, momentarily splitting the enemy forces and dealing significant damage.

The party fought valiantly. Aeli executed a berserker with a brutal stab to the back. Athun, enlarging to a giant size, became a towering battlefield presence, poking his halberd through the doorway with devastating effect. Lars weathered the storm, his attacks methodical and powerful. The battle reached a climax when the leader landed a critical blow on Marcus with his greataxe, a hit so powerful it shattered the wizard’s concentration and collapsed the ice wall. Grievously wounded, Marcus retaliated by filling the room with a devastating **Ice Storm**, pelting the remaining foes with hail and freezing winds. The combination of the party’s relentless assault and the magical barrage finally felled the last berserker. The direwolf, sensing the tide had turned, fled through the fissure in the wall with a final, fading howl.

The presence of these berserkers confirmed that the Amber Temple was not just haunted by the undead, but also served as a base or sacred site for the savage tribes that roamed the slopes of Mount Ghakis. Their motives were unclear—were they guardians, scavengers, or worshippers of the dark powers within?

In the aftermath, the party looted the greataxes of the fallen warriors and listened to the fading howl of the direwolf. They now stood at a crossroads, both literal and metaphorical. A door to the north stood where the berserker leader had fled, while the fissure to the south promised a chase into the wolf's den.

The Amber Temple had begun to collect its price, and it was far from done with them.

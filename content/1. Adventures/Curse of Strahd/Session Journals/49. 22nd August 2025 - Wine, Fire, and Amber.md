## Summary

The wind clawed at them as they woke, sharp and merciless, the kind of cold that sank into bone and whispered of endings. The mountain pass stretched out in both directions, barren and scoured, and above it all loomed the dark reminder of Barovia’s cruel design.
</p><p>
The debate began almost immediately.
</p><p>
“The temple again?” Lars asked, tugging his cloak tight around him. His breath fogged the air. “We nearly died in there. Repeatedly.”
</p><p>
“Kasimir’s sister,” Aeli countered. Her voice was hoarse, but firm. “If there’s even a chance to rescue her, we should try. She might be our only leverage against Strahd.”
</p><p>
Athun muttered to himself, words slipping out like steam through cracked stone. The staff at his side pulsed faintly, hungering.
</p><p>
Marcus held up a hand, ending the argument before it grew teeth. “Doesn’t matter what we decide if that Roc tears us apart before we get two steps off this pass.” His expression softened, almost, almost, into something like humour. “Fortunately, I remembered something. We still have forty-four pounds of goat meat.”
</p><p>
“Forty-four?” Aeli blinked. “Who counts that?”
</p><p>
“I do,” Lars said immediately. “Of course I do.”
</p><p>
The plan formed in fits and starts. Meat, bait, and Athun’s “inspired” idea: soaking the offering in a bottle of Purple Grape Mash No. 3. Wine so sour it could strip rust from iron.
</p><p>
So it was that Lars found himself crouched atop the crumbling tower, arranging wet, purple-stained chunks of meat like some grisly chef. The sky remained clear. Empty. No Roc. The party retreated inside and waited. And waited.
</p><p>
Finally, Marcus shoved his chair back. “Enough. We’re leaving.”
</p><p>
They braced for talons. For wings blotting the sky. For death.
</p><p>
But nothing came.
</p><p>
The pass lay quiet as they descended. Their journey back to the Amber Temple was strangely swift, as though the mountain itself wanted them gone.
</p><p>
The grand hall greeted them with its usual menace: the towering statue wreathed in black mist, and the bobbing green lights of Flameskulls hovering in the corridors like hateful sentries.
</p><p>
“Left wing nearly killed us,” Marcus said. “Right wing it is.”
</p><p>
Aeli uncorked the flask of holy water from Krezk and tightened her grip. The memory of fireball scars was still fresh.
</p><p>
The skulls noticed them almost immediately, their jaws cracking open in eerie laughter. Fire blossomed. The air seared. A Fireball engulfed the hall, tossing them like leaves before a gale.
</p><p>
“Still totally fine,” Lars coughed, singed hair smoking.
</p><p>
Aeli charged, splashing holy water onto the nearest skull. It shrieked and crumbled instantly. Lars actually nodded in approval. “Clever. Didn’t think you had it in you.”
</p><p>
Marcus unleashed an Ice Storm, pelting the hall with frozen shards. The skulls dodged, the cold less effective than hoped, but the bludgeoning force rattled them. Athun struck with his staff, only for its curse to bite back. His eyes went wide, and he turned to flee, muttering, “It’s fine. I’m fine,” as terror drove him away.
</p><p>
But together, Aeli’s blade, Lars’s mace, Athun’s reluctant strikes, they brought the skulls down. Their green light dimmed, leaving only shattered bone and glittering gemstones in their wake.
</p><p>
Athun scooped the gems into a pouch, eyes distant.
</p><p>
The next chamber was bare but for the jackal-headed statue of cracked amber. It stared down at them with lifeless eyes.
</p><p>
And then it moved.
</p><p>
Fists the size of boulders swung. The floor shuddered. Lars caught the first blow, the impact rattling his bones and dropping him to one knee.
</p><p>
Athun roared, slamming his staff into the floor. Fiery shackles burst upward, wrapping the golem in chains of living flame. The construct writhed, restrained, but the staff laughed at him, cursed him, dragged him down into madness. He collapsed, weeping, laughing, screaming, useless on the ground.
</p><p>
“Figures,” Aeli muttered, drawing the Sun Sword. Its radiant blade flared, filling the chamber with golden light. She darted forward, each strike leaving searing gouges in the amber. Lars followed with brutal precision, hammer slamming into fractured joints.
</p><p>
At last, Lars landed a crushing blow, and the golem shattered into amber shards.
</p><p>
They barred the doors, their breathing ragged. Athun’s laughter eventually quieted, and Aeli forced the group upright again with her presence, rallying them with sheer stubbornness.
</p><p>
During the rest, she flipped through Van Richten’s journal, eyes narrowing at a line of cramped script. “He was right. Madam Eva… she’s Strahd’s half-sister.”
</p><p>
The words hung heavy. Even Barovia’s mysteries bled together in twisted bloodlines.
</p><p>
Their path led them to a dusty corridor and down a dark staircase, where the air grew thin.
</p><p>
The smell came first. Rot, rancid and wet, crawling into their lungs and clinging.
</p><p>
Ghasts.
</p><p>
They poured from a side chamber, claws scraping stone, their stench overwhelming. Aeli gagged, forcing herself to stand her ground as one leapt for Lars.
</p><p>
Claws raked him down, blood spattering the wall. He hit the ground hard. For a heartbeat, it looked like he wouldn’t rise again.
</p><p>
“Not today,” Aeli growled, forcing healing light into him, yanking him back from the brink.
</p><p>
The battle pressed hard. Ghasts swarmed the walls and ceiling, their claws reaching from every angle. Marcus steadied his staff, lightning sparking between his fingers. He released a bolt that tore through the hallway, searing half the creatures at once. The smell of charred flesh overpowered even the stench of rot.
</p><p>
Aeli finished the fight with brutal efficiency, tripping one mid-climb. It fell, screeching, and she silenced it with a downward strike of the Sun Sword.
</p><p>
The lair held three amber sarcophagi, each pulsing faintly with unnatural power.
</p><p>
Marcus stepped forward without hesitation, hands brushing the northernmost.
</p><p>
The world vanished.
</p><p>
Fog closed in. A robed figure appeared, its voice cold silk. “I am Drizlash. Imprisoned by the Dark One. Free me, and I will grant you power.”
</p><p>
Its form twisted, revealing the truth: a spider the size of a wolf, legs of bone, mandibles dripping venom.
</p><p>
Marcus didn’t flinch. “Power first.”
</p><p>
The bargain was struck.
</p><p>
When his vision returned, only a second had passed. To the others, he merely touched the sarcophagus. To Marcus, an eternity had whispered.
</p><p>
He opened the lid. A wind screamed outward, carrying a shadowy mist that fled the chamber. Power surged through him, strength, agility, the ability to climb walls like the spider itself.
</p><p>
And then the cost.
</p><p>
A new eye opened on his right cheek, unblinking, inhuman.
</p><p>
Aeli stared. Lars swore. Athun just muttered, “Of course. Of course it would be him.”
</p><p>
The party stood among shattered amber and smoking corpses, their wounds deep, their choices deeper still. One more bargain had been struck. And Barovia would never let them forget it.
</p>

## Summary - Draft


The party awoke in the frigid, wind-scoured pass of the mountains. The biting cold was a stark reminder of their precarious position in Barovia. A debate ensued on their next move, with the primary options being to return to the dreaded Amber Temple or to seek out Kasimir's sister. The noble goal of a rescue mission won out, but a significant obstacle remained: the colossal roc that patrolled the skies near the tower.

Marcus, ever pragmatic, recalled the substantial amount of goat meat harvested from their previous encounter. Lars, upon confirming he was indeed carrying the 44 pounds of meat, proposed a plan: bait the beast. The idea was to place the meat on the tower's roof as a distraction, allowing the party to slip away while the bird was occupied. Athun, in a moment of inspired genius, suggested marinating the offering in his bottle of low-quality "Purple Grape Mash No. 3" wine. The plan, was set in motion.

Lars stealthily arranged the wine-soaked piles of meat on the tower roof under a clear, bird-less sky. The party then retreated inside to wait. After what felt like an eternity of tense anticipation with no sign of the roc, their patience wore thin. Frustrated, Marcus declared enough was enough and simply walked out the front door, the rest of the party scrambling to follow suit.

To their immense relief, their trek back to the Amber Temple was uneventful and, strangely, much quicker than before, taking only an hour through the harsh mountain snows.

Returning to the temple's foreboding entrance, the party stood once more in the grand hall. Before them, the statue topped with a malevolent black mist waited, and the tell-tale green flames of Flameskulls bobbed in the hallways to both the left and right. Remembering the trouble on the left side, they opted to tackle the right wing first. Aeli made preparations, ensuring her flask of holy water from the springs of Krezk was easily accessible.

As they moved through the door, the Flameskulls hovering in the northern hallway turned their fiery gaze upon the intruders. Combat was joined.

**Combat: The Flameskulls of the North Hall**
The battle was a chaotic exchange of fire and force. The skulls proved cunning, focusing their magical assaults on the party.
*   **Enemy Strategy:** The skulls hung back, using their ranged spell attacks. One unleashed a devastating **Fireball** that scorched the entire party, a near-crippling blow that immediately put everyone on the back foot.
*   **Major Attacks & Revelations:** Aeli had a moment of brilliance, using her **holy water** to instantly destroy one skull, a tactic that drew genuine, rare praise from Lars. Marcus unleashed an **Ice Storm**, pelting the undead with hail, though the cold seemed less effective than the bludgeoning. Athun, after striking a skull, was cursed by his own weapon and spent a critical moment **frightened** and fleeing. Ultimately, a combination of Aeli’s holy water, Athun’s staff, and Lars’s relentless melee assaults put the fiery guardians down for good.
*   **Lore Discovery:** Athun collected the gems from the skulls' remains, a grisly but potentially useful trophy.

Pressing on, the party entered a new chamber to the west. The room was bare stone, with collapsed walls to the east. In its center stood a ten-foot-tall statue of a jackal-headed warrior crafted from cracked amber. As they entered, the statue animated, clenching its fists and turning to face them.

**Combat: The Amber Golem**
The golem was a formidable opponent, its amber fists hitting with crushing force.
*   **Near-Death Moments:** Lars bore the brunt of its assault, taking a hit that brought him perilously close to falling. Athun, in a desperate move, used his rune-etched staff to wreathe the golem in **fiery shackles**, restraining it. However, the staff's magic backfired horribly, and Athun was cursed to be **incapacitated** for three minutes, screaming, laughing, and weeping uncontrollably on the floor.
*   **Victory:** With the golem restrained and Athun out of commission, Aeli and Lars focused their attacks. Aeli’s **Sun Sword** shone brightly, each strike carving radiant damage into the amber construct. Lars, fighting with advantage against the restrained target, landed a crushing blow that finally shattered the golem into inert pieces.

With the immediate threat gone, the party barred the doors and took a short, uneasy rest. Athun eventually recovered from his fit, and Aeli used her inspiring presence to rally everyone, granting them temporary hit points. During the rest, Aeli perused **Van Richten's Journal**, piecing together the vampire hunter's rambling notes. She confirmed a crucial piece of lore they had discovered sessions prior: **Madam Eva is Strahd's half-sister**, a revelation that deepened the mysterious history of the von Zarovich bloodline.

Their path led them to a dusty corridor and down a dark staircase, where the air grew thin and heavy with the **stench of death**. The source of the odor was soon revealed as a pack of ghasts, foul, undead creatures, surged from a nearby room to attack.

**Combat: The Ghast Pack**
The fight in the cramped, rubble-strewn hall was a brutal melee.
*   **Enemy Strategy:** The ghasts used their mobility to climb walls and ceilings, swarming the party. Their most dangerous weapon was their **stench**, which forced the party to make constant Constitution saves to avoid being poisoned.
*   **Near-Death Moments:** The ghasts' claws found their mark on Lars, whose health was already depleted from the previous fight. The combined attacks **downed the ranger**, leaving him bleeding on the cold stone floor. Aeli was quick to use her healing ability to get him back on his feet before he could be finished off.
*   **Victory:** The battle was turned by Marcus's strategic use of **Lightning Bolt**, which severely damaged multiple ghasts at once. Aeli’s precise swordwork and a well-timed **Trip Attack** that sent a climbing ghast falling to its doom finally cleared the room.

The ghasts' lair was a chamber with dark green marble floors and three amber sarcophagi set into alcoves. Without hesitation, Marcus approached the northernmost one and laid his hands upon it.

The moment he touched it, his vision vanished. He found himself on a fog-shrouded demiplane, face to face with a robed figure who identified itself as **Drizlash**. It claimed to have been trapped by "the dark one", and offered Marcus a gift in exchange for freedom: the power to **spider climb**. The entity revealed its true form: a monstrous, wolf-sized spider with jointed, bony legs. Marcus, intrigued by the power offered, agreed. The vision ended as suddenly as it began.

To the rest of the party, only a second had passed. Marcus then pushed open the sarcophagus lid. A gust of wind and a blinding light erupted from within, followed by a dark, shadowy mist that fled up the stairs and out of the temple. Marcus felt the new power course through him, but it came with a physical cost: a new, unblinking **eye opened on his right cheek**.

The session ended with the party wounded but victorious, standing amidst the evidence of their struggles, now bound deeper to the dark forces at play in Barovia through the bargain one of them had willingly made.

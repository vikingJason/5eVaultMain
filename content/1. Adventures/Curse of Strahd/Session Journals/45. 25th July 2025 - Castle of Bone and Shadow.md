## Summary

<p>
The storm clawed at the castle, as if it too longed to tear down Ravenloft stone by stone.
</p><p>
Rain sheeted the ramparts where the companions walked, half-blinded by wind and shadow. Lightning revealed jagged mountain silhouettes beyond the battlements, and below, a village huddled in gloom. Lars leaned over the slick stone edge, his cloak soaked and useless.
</p><p>
“I see nothing but ghosts and mist,” he muttered.
</p><p>
Aeli’s sword pulsed faintly in her grip, casting a radius of holy light, thin and fragile against the darkness, but stubborn in its glow.
</p><p>
They moved quickly, avoiding the path of a patrolling suit of armor. It was too soon since their last battle. Too soon for more wounds.
</p><p>
Athun spotted the trapdoor first. Beneath it, a tight stairwell spiralled into another turret, mirroring the one they had escaped near the organ room. Down they went, unaware they were descending not just through stone, but into the bones of the castle itself.
</p><p>
The stench hit first: decay, iron, and wet mould.
</p><p>
A chamber unfolded before them, filled with rotting cots and rust-flecked weapons. Yellow lichen bloomed across the ceiling like a sickness too stubborn to die. Lars ran a gloved hand across it, sniffed his fingers, and grunted. “Harmless,” he announced. “Unlike everything else here.”
</p><p>
Then the bones moved.
</p><p>
Skeletons rose from the detritus of the fallen, once guards, now twisted echoes. Their empty sockets flared with false life. Marcus stepped back, fire already forming at his fingertips, while Aeli surged forward, sword gleaming like a shard of sunlight.
</p><p>
Athun, ever the bulwark, summoned the runes branded into his soul. Flames burst from his axe, searing through brittle ribcages. But Lars grunted as a rusted blade scraped across his side, its taint venomous, poisoning his blood.
</p><p>
By the end, bone fragments littered the floor. The silence returned, uneasy.
</p><p>
Among the ruin, Aeli claimed a shield. Athun discarded his cursed axe for a simpler blade. Nothing felt like victory here, only survival, rented by the minute.
</p><p>
The next door opened into madness.
</p><p>
A room sculpted entirely of bone. Ceilings arched with femurs and skulls. Chairs made from vertebrae circled a long, bleached table. And above it all, a chandelier of human and elven skulls, their grins cracked and eternal.
</p><p>
A dragon’s skull, massive, hollow-eyed, rested near a set of sealed double doors. Aeli stumbled back at the sight, her sword dimming.
</p><p>
Her connection to her patron, to the threads of arcane power that whispered through her blood... vanished.
</p><p>
As if the very bones mocked her.
</p><p>
Her gaze lingered on the dragon’s skull. Argynvost, whispered a voice in her memory. It didn’t matter how she knew it, it was him. She swore silently to return him to his resting place. Or make Strahd pay for the desecration.
</p><p>
They left the bone cathedral through the northern passage, unwilling to face whatever lingered behind the eastern doors.
</p><p>
They opened the door where Vidar waited for them like a spider in a lacquered web.
</p><p>
The now servant of Strahd, lounged behind an ornate desk. Candles flickered, casting angular shadows on racks lined with weapons bearing the crest of Barovia. He tapped the lid of a chest beside him. Stolen gear. Seized weapons. Power held just out of reach.
</p><p>
“You’ve added to my collection,” he said, voice like glass over gravel. “My master told me how easily he crushed you. Have you reconsidered his offer?”
</p><p>
No reply came. Only Lars, bloodied, unrelenting, charged.
</p><p>
Steel clashed. Magic sparked. Vidar vanished with a twist of light, Misty Step, and reappeared, blade wreathed in emerald fire. He was graceful, taunting, almost gleeful.
</p><p>
“You won’t survive this,” he hissed, parrying blow after blow. “But don’t let that stop you from trying.”
</p><p>
Yet the tide turned. Athun swung with fury, his fire rune blazing. Marcus’s spells missed often, but his defiance struck true.
</p><p>
Vidar, faced with rising resistance, vanished once more. This time, for good.
</p><p>
The chest burst open, and their weapons were reclaimed. The Sun Sword ignited in Aeli’s hand like dawn remembered. 
</p><p>
They barricaded the study with desks and bent wood. Lars, ever the gambler, shoved his own longsword into the door’s gears, breaking the lock.
</p><p>
Aeli opened the Tome of Strahd, pages whispering secrets too dense to grasp quickly. A warning about the land. Rituals. A history bathed in blood.
</p><p>
But they had no time.
</p><p>
Lars, without hesitation, dropped Vidar’s journal into the fire.
</p><p>
“Wait!” Marcus began, hand outstretched.
</p><p>
Too late. Flame consumed words they would never read.
</p><p>
“Nothing worth keeping,” Lars said, not looking back.
</p><p>
As they prepared to rest, metal footsteps echoed down the hall.
</p><p>
“Lars!” Vidar called, his voice playfully cruel. “Come out and play.”
</p><p>
They did not answer. They did not open the door.
</p><p>
Instead, Lars found a hidden panel, a staircase, old and crumbling, revealed by dust and instinct. They fled into it, sealing the passage behind.
</p><p>
The air changed.
</p><p>
Mist rose from the stones, thick and suffocating. It curled into form, tall, regal, fanged.
</p><p>
Strahd.
</p><p>
“You have worn out your welcome,” he said. “Your gods cannot save you now.”
</p><p>
Aeli didn’t hesitate. She thrust the Sun Sword forward. It passed through him, slicing nothing.
</p><p>
Strahd’s image wavered, melting like wax. He laughed, soft, full of promise, and vanished into the stones.
</p><p>
And Now...
</p><p>
They stood in darkness, lit only by one sword’s flickering glow. Bruised. Bloodied. Betrayed. But not broken.
</p><p>
They had escaped Vidar’s clutches, uncovered a piece of Strahd’s lair, and reclaimed what was stolen. And though they were deep in the beast’s den, there was still breath in their lungs.
</p><p>
In Barovia, that was a kind of victory.
</p><p>
The castle would not give them another so easily.
</p>

## Summary 2

Rain whispered against stone as the group stood upon the narrow parapet encircling Castle Ravenloft. The village below was a smear of shadows through the downpour, unrecognizable. A low wind tugged at cloaks and whispered through broken battlements, bringing with it the scent of decay, mildew, and something fouler. Something ancient.

Lars gripped the parapet edge, eyes narrowed, his breath visible in the cold.

Aeli didn’t answer. She had felt it the moment they crossed the castle threshold—a faltering in her bond, a shiver in the thread that connected her to her patron. Power still coursed through her veins, yes, but it flickered like candlelight caught in a draft.

They moved on, silent save for the creak of old boards and the soft clink of gear. Down a cobweb-strewn hallway. Past patrolling armor they dared not disturb. Into a forgotten guardroom, where time had left only brittle bones and hollow helmets.

But the true horror waited below.

---

### **The Ossuary**

The door groaned as it opened, revealing a room that should not be. The walls were crafted of bone—**actual bone**—polished white and fit together with horrifying precision. Femurs lined the archways. Ribs curled into decorative spirals. The chandelier above them was a cage of spines, and upon the eastern doors hung a massive **dragon skull**, its eye sockets empty yet somehow _watchful_.

Aeli froze. Her sword arm trembled.

“Argynvost,” she whispered. “I think this was... him.”

The name lingered in the room like an accusation. Her warlock magic hummed erratically, as if the very presence of the bones disrupted it. She clutched at the waning thread of power.

A sound. Bones shifting.

Then they rose.

Skeletons burst from bone-crafted furniture, their eye sockets aglow with pale blue fire. The room erupted in chaos.

---

### **The Battle**

Aeli danced into the fray, her sword blazing in a wide arc, slicing bone from bone. Athun invoked his rune—fire leapt from his hands and scorched three of the undead into dust. Marcus shouted an incantation and launched a firebolt through a ribcage, sending a skull spinning across the room.

But Lars stumbled, gasping. Blood ran down his arm from a cut made earlier—barely a scratch, he’d thought—but the blade had been rusted. Now poison dulled his movements. He slashed clumsily, gritting his teeth through the haze.

They won, but not without pain.

In the silence afterward, they scavenged what little they could. A rusted short sword for Athun. A battered shield Aeli slung over her back. The rest—junk. The room’s darkness still clung to them like oil.

---

### **Vidar’s Study**

They found him where arrogance always waits—in a study of polished wood, aged parchment, and cruel certainty.

**Vidar** stood behind a desk, firelight casting shadows across his sharp features. His crimson cloak curled around him like smoke.

“Well,” he drawled, fingers steepled, “you made it farther than I expected. Yesterhill didn’t break you? Pity.”

On the far side of the room, a chest sat open. Their gear lay inside—**the Sun Sword**, the **Tome of Strahd**.

Lars didn’t hesitate.

He _lunged_, poison forgotten. His blade sparked against Vidar’s flaming scimitar, and the room erupted in violence. Marcus threw spells, Aeli struck from behind, and Athun moved with brutal efficiency.

But Vidar was no ordinary foe. He slipped through shadows, blinked across the room in bursts of arcane mist, his blade dancing like a serpent on fire. He laughed through the pain. _Enjoyed_ it.

They retrieved their gear. The Sun Sword pulsed with warmth in Aeli’s hand.

But Vidar escaped—again.

---

### **Barricades And Mockery**

They barricaded the door with overturned shelves and a desk. Marcus drew protective sigils. Lars, his anger still smoldering, found Vidar’s journal and tossed it into the hearth without a second thought. The flames devoured it greedily.

Aeli flipped through the **Tome of Strahd**, pages brittle with age. She read about the castle’s secret paths, about heartbreak and betrayal. About gods forgotten.

Then came the voice—_mocking, theatrical_.

**“Lars! Come out and play!”**

The door shook under Vidar’s blows, laughter echoing like a child’s rhyme turned sour.

---

### **Into The Mist**

They found the hidden panel by accident—Athun tracing the grain of the wooden wall as he muttered about airflow. A click. A shift.

Behind the study: a spiral stair, cloaked in shadow.

As they descended, a **mist** pooled ahead of them. Thick. Cold. It began to take shape. Coalesce. Form.

A figure stood in their path, regal and terrible.

**Strahd.**

His voice was silk over steel: _“You have worn out your welcome. Whatever gods you believe in cannot save you now.”_

Aeli did not wait.

The Sun Sword sang through the air, a ribbon of radiant fire—and passed through him. No resistance.

An illusion.

And yet the air still felt colder. Heavier. _Hungrier._

---

### **Reflections And Dread**

They did not speak. They only descended, deeper into the castle. Into whatever lay below.

The ossuary had shown them the castle's cruelty.

Vidar had shown them its cunning.

And Strahd—whether illusion or not—had shown them the _inevitability_ of their meeting.

Each step down the spiral stair felt like the turn of a page they could not unread.

They were in his story now.

And they were running out of chapters.

---

**End of Session Hook:**  
What secrets lie below the castle’s bones? Will Vidar confront them again—or was that just a distraction from something worse? With the Tome of Strahd in hand and the Sun Sword ignited, the time for hiding is ending. The castle is waking. And it remembers its enemies.

The party—**Lars (Barth)**, **Aeli (Ailey)**, **Athun**, and **Marcus**—finds themselves in **Castle Ravenloft** after defeating animated armor guarding the keep. The session is filled with exploration, combat, and tense encounters, culminating in a confrontation with **Vidar**, Strahd’s servant, and an eerie illusion of Strahd himself.

- The party stands on a rain-soaked walkway encircling the keep, observing a village below but failing to recognize landmarks due to poor visibility.

- A **dragon skull** (later identified as **Argynvost’s**) is mounted on the eastern doors, radiating sorrow and dread.
- Aeli feels her **warlock patron connection waver** in this room, hinting at its dark power.

### **3. Combat with Skeletons**

- The skeletal remains animate and attack. The party fights them off, with:
		- **Aeli** dealing significant damage with her sword.
		- **Athun** using his fire rune to destroy skeletons.
		- **Lars** struggling due to poison (from a rusty sword nick) but contributing.
		- **Marcus** landing a firebolt.
- The party loots **rusted weapons** (mostly unusable) but retrieves a **shield** (Aeli) and a **short sword** (Athun).

### **4. Confrontation with Vidar**

- The party enters **Vidar’s study**, finding him at a desk. He taunts them, referencing their past defeat at Yesterhill and revealing their stolen gear in a chest.
- **Lars instigates combat**, attacking Vidar. The fight is tense:
		- Vidar proves elusive, dodging most attacks and using **Misty Step** to escape.
		- He wields a **flaming scimitar**, dealing heavy damage.
		- The party **retrieves their gear** (including the **Sun Sword** and **Tome of Strahd**) before Vidar flees again.

### **5. Barricading and Rest**

- The party attempts to **barricade themselves** in the study for a short rest.
		- Lars burns **Vidar’s journal** without reading it.
		- Aeli reads **Strahd’s Tome** for clues about the castle.
- Vidar returns, pounding on the door and mocking them: *"Lars! Come out and play!"*

### **6. Secret Passage and Illusionary Strahd**

- The party discovers a **hidden staircase** behind a panel in the study.
- As they descend, a **mist coalesces into Strahd’s form**, who declares: *"You have worn out your welcome. Whatever gods you believe in cannot save you now."*
- The party attacks, only to realize it’s an **illusion** (revealed when Aeli’s Sun Sword passes through him).

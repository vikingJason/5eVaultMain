## Summary


The first warning was the smoke.
</p><p>
It curled up the cellar stairs in a thick grey plume, creeping into the Abbey’s dining hall as the party stumbled out of the firestorm they themselves had unleashed. The air tasted metallic, bitter with burning varnish and splintered wood. Beneath it, the stone groaned with the first hints of collapse.
</p><p>
Marcus pinched the bridge of his nose. “Okay,” he said, voice dry as old parchment. “I think it’s time to get out of here.”
</p><p>
No one argued.
</p><p>
They ran, boots splashing through puddles, cloaks whipping behind them, until at last they spilled into the courtyard overlooking Krezk. Morning had come, but Barovia’s eternal haze turned the sun into a pale, blurred coin. The Abbey burned behind them, slow and inevitable.
</p><p>
That was when the *sound* began.
</p><p>
A distant, rhythmic *thud*, deep, ponderous, wrong. Then the forest far below rustled in a rolling wave, trees swaying aside as though something massive moved through them.
</p><p>
Dread settled over the group like a damp cloak.
</p><p>
Lars knelt, closing his eyes. He reached outward with primal instinct, searching the miles around them. Within seconds he paled.
</p><p>
“Something’s coming,” he whispered. “Something big.”
</p><p>
Aeli glanced back at the Abbey, flames licking higher. “Our options,” she said, “are slimming while we speak.”
</p><p>
They turned from the fire and descended toward the one place in Krezk that had granted them safety before: the blessed pool at the village’s heart.
</p><p>
Tremors followed them down the hillside, small at first, then growing, beating a grim cadence beneath their feet.
</p><p>
Their plan formed fast, desperate, and strangely brilliant.
</p><p>
The pool was holy. Holy water hurt undead. And whatever was coming could not be worse than facing the unknown on open ground.
</p><p>
Lars filled his waterskin with the shimmering liquid, while Marcus, paranoid ingenuity sparking, ransacked a nearby shed. He emerged with a bucket, then promptly scaled the town wall like a disgruntled insect, positioning his prize with careful aim.
</p><p>
“Holy-water bombardment,” he muttered to himself. “Perfectly reasonable.”
</p><p>
They waited.
</p><p>
But the creature marching toward them did not arrive.
</p><p>
Something else did.
</p><p>
A familiar black wagon creaked into town, the same one Vidar had fled in. It rolled slowly toward the burning Abbey, its wooden wheels whispering over the gravel.
</p><p>
Lars didn’t hesitate. Subtlety was never his domain. One arrow thudded into the wagon’s side.
</p><p>
It stopped.
</p><p>
Silence.
</p><p>
Marcus sighed. “Marcus will just hurl a fireball at it.”
</p><p>
The explosion lit the morning like a second sunrise. Flames roared up around the wagon, only to gutter away, leaving the wood untouched.
</p><p>
Aeli raised an eyebrow. “This is some voodoo bullshit.”
</p><p>
Lars shattered a window with a second arrow.
</p><p>
The wagon shuddered… and the fight began.
</p><p>
The party unleashed everything they had.
</p><p>
Ice rained down from Marcus’s conjured storm, battering the roof. Lars fired arrow after arrow. Aeli’s Sun Sword glimmered in her hand, anticipation humming along the blade.
</p><p>
It was Athun who ended the standoff.
</p><p>
He strode forward through shards of melting ice and, with a single brutal swing, split the wagon door down the centre.
</p><p>
Inside were three coffins. And a chest.
</p><p>
The coffins burst open.
</p><p>
Strahd’s brides spilled out, undead monstrosities in tattered wedding gowns, eyes burning like coals. More specter than woman, each moved with hungry purpose.
</p><p>
One launched at Athun, fangs sinking deep. His strength ebbed instantly, muscles trembling. Another grappled with Aeli, only to recoil as radiant light scorched its flesh.
</p><p>
Marcus, blasting one apart with *Magic Missile*, shouted triumphantly, “STAAAKE!”  
The new battle cry was born.
</p><p>
A whirl of magic and steel followed. Marcus transformed one of the brides into a helpless tortoise, flailing uselessly on its back. Lars tore a spoke from the wagon wheel Athun had broken, then drove it through the immobilized creature. Aeli followed through with a radiant decapitation, dissolving it into ash.
</p><p>
The others fell soon after, fire, sunlight, and steel overwhelming them in a storm of brutality.
</p><p>
When the final bride crumbled, silence returned.
</p><p>
Aeli knelt beside scattered ashes. “Stake, decapitate, burn,” she recited. “But the Sun Sword handles all that neatly.”
</p><p>
They moved to the chest.
</p><p>
Athun avoided a volley of poison darts with practiced ease, then opened the lid.
</p><p>
Inside: a collection of Strahd’s macabre treasures. A wedding veil woven from mist. A painting of Ireena that seemed to shift when not observed. Silver and obsidian jewellery meant for a bride.
</p><p>
A card lay atop the items.
</p><p>
*To my beloved Tatyana.
</p><p>
No one spoke for a long moment.
</p><p>
The ground trembled.
</p><p>
Not subtly. Not distantly.  
Now with the force of something enormous approaching fast.
</p><p>
When the party turned toward the gate, their breaths caught.
</p><p>
A colossal tree, its bark riddled with black veins of necrotic light, strode through the forest like a titan. Branches swayed with unnatural sinew. Roots snapped and reformed as it walked.
</p><p>
They knew that shape.
</p><p>
The Gulthias Tree.
</p><p>
But grown monstrously beyond their last encounter.
</p><p>
It marched toward Krezk with terrible purpose.
</p><p>
Decisions crashed over them like cold water.
</p><p>
Lars hefted his axe. “Holy water won’t help against a tree,” he said, already moving toward the gate.
</p><p>
Marcus, Athun, and Aeli exchanged looks, then retreated toward the blessed pool, holy water still their only reliable tool against undead forces, should more arrive.
</p><p>
Above them, the sky dimmed further, as though even the sun feared to witness what came next.
</p><p>
The colossal tree descended upon Krezk.
</p><p>
And the party braced for two catastrophes at once, one of wood and rot, the other of fire and obsession, each threatening to remake the village before the day’s end.

## Summary - Draft


The acrid scent of smoke was the first herald of their new predicament. As the party stumbled back into the Abbey's dining hall, a thick, grey plume billowed from the cellar stairs behind them. The fire they had set was beginning to claim the ancient structure.

Marcus, ever pragmatic, was the first to voice the obvious. "Okay, I think it's time to get out of here." With little debate, the group fled the burning Abbey, spilling out into the quiet courtyard high above the village of Krezk. The sun was a pale, shrouded disc behind perpetual clouds, casting the world in a muted, early morning light.

It was then that the sound began, a distant, rhythmic *thud*, followed by the unsettling rustle of trees far below, as if something immense was parting the forest with each step. A sense of dread settled over them. Lars, attempting to identify the threat, sat and concentrated, using his primal awareness to sense the presence of evils within a mile. The answer was a resounding and terrifying *yes*; something powerful and unnatural was approaching.

Debating their options, they ruled out a suicidal charge into the mist-shrouded woods. Athun, ever cautious, argued for a defensible position. Aeli, watching the Abbey burn, noted dryly, "I think our options are slimming while we speak." They settled on a retreat to the one place in Krezk that had offered them sanctuary and power: the shimmering, blessed pool at the town's center.

As they hurried down the path, the tremors grew stronger. The plan was simple: use the pool's holy water as a weapon. Lars filled his waterskin, while Marcus, in a stroke of paranoid genius, ransacked a nearby shed for a bucket, scaled the town wall using his spider-like abilities, and positioned his own bucket of holy water as a makeshift artillery piece.

They didn't have to wait long. The rhythmic crunch of gravel announced a new arrival, but not the one they expected. From the road emerged a familiar, foreboding black wagon, the very same Vidar had used to escape them once before. It moved with an unhurried, deliberate pace towards the burning Abbey.

Lars, never one for subtlety when an arrow would do, loosed a shot that thudded into the wagon's side. It halted. Silence. Marcus, his pyromaniacal passions ignited, didn't hesitate. "Marcus will just hurl a fireball at it." The wagon was engulfed in a roaring inferno, but when the flames died down, it stood utterly untouched. "This is some voodoo bullshit," Aeli muttered. Lars shattered a window with an arrow, and the fight was on.

The party unleashed a volley of attacks. Marcus called down an Ice Storm that pelted the area, cracking the wagon's windows. Arrows from Lars clattered off the enchanted wood. It was Athun, eschewing magic for pure, brute force, who ended the stalemate. He strode forward and with a mighty blow from his greatsword, he split the wagon door in twain, revealing three coffins and a chest inside.

The wagon doors burst open, not with Vidar, but with Strahd's spawn, each a terrifying vision in a tattered wedding gown, their eyes burning with crimson hunger. One latched onto Athun, her fangs sinking deep into his neck, draining his vitality and permanently reducing his strength. Another went for Aeli, who fought back with the radiant power of the Sun Sword, its holy light searing the vampire's flesh. "STAAAKE!" Marcus yelled after felling one with a volley of Magic Missiles, establishing the party's new battle cry.

Seeking to control the fray, Marcus used Polymorph on one of the brides, transforming her into a helpless tortoise. The party seized the advantage. Lars, using a spoke from the wagon wheel Athun had damaged earlier, expertly staked the paralyzed tortoise. Aeli, following up with the Sun Sword, cleanly decapitated the creature, which crumbled to ash. The remaining brides were overwhelmed by a combined assault of radiant sword strikes, magical fire, and Athun's relentless blade.

As the last bride fell, Aeli recalled the vampire lore she had learned: a stake only paralyzes, but decapitation and burning are required for true destruction. The Sun Sword's unique power, however, seemed to bypass this, disintegrating its target utterly.

With the immediate threat gone, the party investigated the wagon's chest. It was trapped, but Athun deftly avoided a volley of poison darts. Inside, they found a trove of macabre gifts, including a card that read, "To my beloved Tatiana." The contents, a wedding veil woven from mist, a magical painting of Ireena, a ring of silver and obsidian, and other trinkets, were a chilling testament to Strahd's obsession with his lost love and his claim over her reincarnation, Ireena.

The distant thuds they had heard earlier intensified, shaking the very ground. To their horror, they saw its source: a gigantic, walking tree, its eyes glowing with a necrotic green light, was marching relentlessly towards Krezk's gates. They recognized it, the Gulthias Tree from Yester Hill, but it had grown immensely larger since their last encounter.

The party split their strategy. Lars, convinced the holy water would be useless against an evil plant, hefted his axe and moved to confront the tree head-on at the gate. Marcus, Aeli, and Athun fell back to the blessed pool, ready to use its waters as both a weapon and a source of healing.

The colossal tree was approaching the gates of Krezk and the party divided, staring down two different apocalypses—one of flame and one of wood.

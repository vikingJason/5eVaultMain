## Summary
<p>
Rain fell softly over Krezk, as though the sky itself were trying to wash the village clean.
</p><p>
It failed.
</p><p>
The blood remained, darkening the earth where the party had found the child werewolf, throat opened, belly split, salt laid with ritual precision. No frenzy. No mercy. Just intent.
</p><p>
They found shelter in a small house spared the worst of the carnage. Furniture lay overturned, but there was no blood. Lars moved immediately to the shutters, testing weak latches and warped wood. “Town walls can stop werewolves,” he muttered, hammering one into place, “but the windows are apparently decorative.”
</p><p>
A fire was lit. The house settled.
</p><p>
Then Marcus stared out into the rain and frowned. The mud outside wasn’t random. It was patterned. Repeated. A face, forming again and again as droplets struck earth.
</p><p>
“Guys,” he said slowly. “The rain’s making a picture.”
</p><p>
Aeli humoured him, at first. But unease crept in, quiet and unwelcome. Athun watched the flames. Shapes moved there. Humanoid. Watching back. 
</p><p>
Lars looked too, and saw his own past carved into firelight. Scratches in walls. Torn fabric. His sister’s dress.
</p><p>
“This house is cursed,” he said.
</p><p>
They rested anyway.
</p><p>
“It’s the best of the terrible options,” Aeli sighed.
</p><p>
---
</p><p>
After a short rest and a grim review of Van Richten’s notes on zombies, the conversation shifted.
</p><p>
Castle Ravenloft felt distant now, not forgotten, but postponed. The land itself was sick, and the pull of unfinished wounds drew them south. Yester Hill. The gemstone. The Fanes.
</p><p>
“We should burn the whole town down,” Marcus offered, far too casually.
</p><p>
No one agreed. No one dismissed it outright either.
</p><p>
The road south was cruel. Sleet hardened into snow. Athun, lacking protection from the cold, felt the Barovian chill bite deep, exhaustion creeping into his bones.
</p><p>
Yester Hill rose ahead, silent, white, waiting.
</p><p>
The great wooden statue of Strahd stood inert. The cavity where the blight-tree had once grown was now nothing but churned earth and rot. The hill was not dead. A voice slid into Lars’s thoughts, deep and ancient.
</p><p>
*"Long have I waited. My spear hungers for blood. Take it. Rule these mountains."*
</p><p>
Drawn to a ritual stone, Lars dug. Bones. Old blood. A spear, stained and patient.
</p><p>
Behind him, Marcus did what Marcus did best. He lit the statue on fire.
</p><p>
The flames burned purple.
</p><p>
The statue melted, not collapsing, but peeling. Beneath Strahd’s likeness stood another form entirely: towering, feminine, carved of wood and stone. Serene. Furious. Awake.
</p><p>
At her feet stood a pedestal, runes etched deep. Athun recognized the script but could not read it. Aeli did.
</p><p>
*“Marcus,”* she said sharply, *“the chalice. The Chalice of the Morning Lord.”*
</p><p>
As she brought it forward, the runes glowed green. So did Marcus’s pack. The Wizard of Wines gemstone answered the call. Marcus panicked. Ran. *“Not again!”*
</p><p>
Aeli tackled him into the snow. *“This is what’s supposed to happen!”*
</p><p>
Together, they placed the gemstone into the chalice. The clouds split. Sunlight poured down upon Yester Hill, real sunlight. Snow melted. Warmth returned. For the first time in Barovia, the sun touched their skin.
</p><p>
The statue moved.
</p><p>
*“You have woken me,”* said the Mountain Fane. *“What do you seek?”*
</p><p>
*“Peace,”* Aeli said, spinning once in the light.
</p><p>
*“And what does peace look like to you?”*
</p><p>
Lars stepped forward. *“The death of Strahd.”*
</p><p>
The storm returned in fury, then faded.
</p><p>
*“He is a blight,”* the Fane said. *“Prove yourselves worthy.”*
</p><p>
The trials were simple. They were not kind.
</p><p>
**A Permanent Scar.** Lars stepped forward without hesitation. Lightning burned a scar into his chest, searing life away. He did not cry out.
**A Shattered Treasure.** Marcus produced his Book of Beasts, years of work. He burned it himself.
**Blood Spilled Willingly.** Athun offered too little. Then more. Still not enough. Understanding dawned. He cut deep, collapsing to the brink of death. The Fane accepted him.
**A Treasured Memory.** Aeli faltered, then chose. A day in her father’s shop. The smell of wood. His voice. Gone. She could no longer remember his face.
</p><p>
Satisfied, the Mountain Fane smiled.
</p><p>
*“You have freed me. The land no longer watches in silence.”*
</p><p>
She sank into the earth, gemstone clutched close, one arm raised defiantly. Green light washed over them, healing, cleansing, complete.
</p><p>
The sun followed them as they left.
</p><p>

They reached the Wizard of Wines by dusk. Davian Martikov stared at the sunlight trailing them like a blessing. When Marcus spoke of seeing the sun, the old man wept.
</p><p>
*“I see hope,”* he whispered.
</p><p>
They spoke of Berez. Davian’s expression darkened.
</p><p>
*“You know Baba Lysaga, and still you go?”* he asked.
</p><p>

</p><p>
*“She is powerful,”* Davian said quietly. *“And she is watching.”*
</p><p>
That night, the party rested in warmth and wine, restored in body, but marked in soul.
</p><p>
Scars burned. Memories were gone.
</p><p>
And ahead waited the witch who helped make the devil they intended to kill.

## Summary - Draft



### A Chronicle of Sunlight and Sacrifice

The Unclean Refuge

The rain began as a subtle drizzle over the corpse-strewn village of Krezk, a soft percussion on the blood-soaked earth. Exhausted and haunted by their discovery—a werewolf child, slit from neck to navel and encircled by a ring of salt—the party sought shelter. They found a modest dwelling untouched by gore, its furniture merely overturned. Inside, Lars methodically secured the weak wooden shutters, muttering about Barovian architectural priorities. "It's like they made the town walls werewolf-proof but forgot the windows."

As he lit a fire, the first signs of the house's strangeness emerged. Marcus, staring out at the downpour, saw not random patterns, but the repeated, hammered image of Strahd von Zarovich's portrait forming in the mud. "Guys... the rain's making a picture." Aeli humored him with theatrical agreement, but a shared unease settled in.

Athun, gazing into the newborn flames, saw fleeting humanoid shapes. Lars, looking into the same fire, was assaulted by memories of his own family's slaughter, scratches on walls, his sister's torn dress. "This house was cursed," he concluded grimly. Despite the omens, they agreed to rest. "It's the best of the terrible options," Aeli sighed, the motto of their Barovian existence.

Whispers from the Hill

A short rest and a study of Van Richten's notes on zombies later, the debate turned to purpose. The immediate goal of returning to Castle Ravenloft had faded. The lure of the Wizard of Wines gemstone and the corrupted Fanes of Barovia pulled them south, to Yesterhill. "We should burn the entire town down," Marcus suggested with unsettling enthusiasm, a glimmer in his eye. "Solves all our problems." The plan was tabled, but the pyromaniacal spark was noted.

The trek was a descent into bitter cold. Sleet turned to snow, and Athun, lacking a protective goat cloak, gained a level of exhaustion, his body wracked by the unnatural chill. They arrived at the silent, snow-dusted hill. The giant wooden statue of Strahd stood inert, the cavity where the gem-powered tree blight had grown now just a mess of upturned earth.

But the hill was not asleep. A deep voice whispered into Lars's mind: *"Long have I waited for one who is worthy. My spear hungers for blood. Retrieve it and rule these mountains in my stead."* Drawn to a ritual stone, Lars dug with a borrowed handaxe, uncovering moldy bones and a blood-stained spear. Meanwhile, Marcus, ever vigilant, decided the Strahd-statue was an affront. He lit it with a torch. It ignited not with normal flame, but with a sickening, necrotic purple fire.

The Awakening of the Mountain

The purple flames melted the statue's outer layer, revealing a towering, serene yet fierce feminine form—a statue of living wood and stone. At its feet was a pedestal etched with runes. Athun recognized the script but could not read it. Aeli, however, had a flash of insight. "Marcus, the chalice! The Chalice of the Morning Lord!"

The runes on the pedestal and the sacred chalice were of the same language, and they began to glow with a resonant green light as Aeli brought them together. A matching light erupted from Marcus's pack—the Wizard of Wines gemstone, the stolen heart of the land, was calling out. Panicked by the familiar glow of magical disaster, Marcus bolted down the hill. "Not again!" Aeli, convinced this was the path to restoration, gave chase and crash-tackled him in the mud. "Look at the gemstone! This is what's *supposed* to happen!"

Convinced, they placed the gemstone within the chalice upon the pedestal. The clouds over Yesterhill parted. For the first time in their Barovian journey, they saw the sun. Its golden light bathed the hill, melting the snow, warming their bones. The statue moved, its eyes blinking open. A voice, both of the earth and the wind, spoke.

*"You have woken me. What is it that you seek here?"*

"Peace," Aeli answered, twirling childishly in the sunlight.

*"And what does peace look like to you?"* the entity, who called itself the Mountain Fane, inquired.

Lars stepped forward. "The death of Strahd." At the name, the Fane's face contorted in rage. The clouds slammed back, lightning striking the earth. As her anger subsided, so did the storm. "That monster is a blight. He has used my power to corrupt the weather itself. To prove you are worthy of my aid in destroying him, you must endure."

She laid out four trials of sacrifice:
1.  A Permanent Scar. Lars stepped forward without hesitation. A bolt of lightning seared a Harry Potter-esque scar onto his chest, permanently reducing his vitality. He bore it without a sound.
2.  A Shattered Treasure. Marcus, with a pained look, produced his life's work—his Book of Beasts. He set it alight, watching years of research turn to ash.
3.  Blood Spilled Willingly. Athun misjudged the scale of the offering, offering drops, then a cut palm. The Fane grew impatient. "Why do you resist what needs to be done?" Realizing she wanted a true brush with death, Athun slashed deeply, bringing himself to the very brink, leaving him with only 1 hit point. The Fane finally nodded, respecting his endurance.
4.  A Treasured Memory Forgone. Aeli, struggling to voice fears, was offered an alternative. She chose a cherished memory of working in her father's shop. As she described it to the Fane, the memory itself was stripped from her mind. She could no longer recall her father's face or name.

Satisfied, the Mountain Fane thanked them. "You have freed me from Strahd's control. I will help you restore this land. The mountain and the hill no longer watch in silence. They watch *with* you." Clutching the purified gemstone to her chest, she sank into the earth, her arm rising in a defiant, thumb-up salute. A pulse of green, revitalizing energy washed over the party, granting them the benefits of a full long rest and cleansing all exhaustion. The patch of sunlight persisted, following them as they left.

A Toast at the Winery

Deciding to shelter at the Wizard of Wines, they arrived at dusk, the strange sunbeam still trailing them. Davian Martikov welcomed them with wine and awe. When Marcus mentioned seeing the sun, a single tear rolled down the old man's cheek. "I see hope on the horizon," he whispered.

They revealed their next destination: the swamp-ruin of Berez. Davian grew grave. "You know of Baba Lysaga, and yet you go willingly?" He shared the local lore: that Baba Lysaga was Strahd's midwife and a second mother, a devout follower of Mother Night who instilled the first spark of magic in him. Jealous, Queen Ravenovia banished her, and Lysaga's dark prayers are said to have cursed the queen to an early death. "She is powerful. Not even divination magic can pierce her shields. Some say she bathes in the blood of beasts."

With those chilling words, he retired for the night, offering them continued hospitality. The party was left in the warm winery, their bodies restored but their minds heavy with new burdens—scars, lost memories, and the daunting knowledge that to free Barovia, they must now confront the witch who helped create its devil.

### **Lore Revelations & Arc Connections**

*   **The Fanes of Barovia:** The party confirmed the existence of the three ancient land spirits (Fanes) and successfully restored one—the **Mountain Fane**. The ritual revealed that Strahd corrupted them, stealing their power to reinforce his own dominion over the land (weather, etc.). Restoring them is key to weakening him.
*   **Baba Lysaga's Origins:** Her deep, familial connection to Strahd was revealed. She is not just a powerful hag, but his magical foster-mother, whose exile and bitterness directly tie into the von Zarovich family tragedy. She possesses the **Holy Symbol of Ravenkind**.
*   **Strahd's Omnipresence:** The rain-portrait was a clear sign of his constant, mocking surveillance. The corruption of the Fanes is a major source of his power, explaining the land's desolation.

### **Loose Ends & Future Hooks**

*   **The Remaining Fanes:** Two Fanes (Swamp and Forest) remain corrupted. Their locations are unknown, but the Swamp Fane is likely tied to Berez.
*   **Baba Lysaga & the Holy Symbol:** The party's next destination holds a key artifact and a deeply personal enemy of Strahd.
*   **The Purified Gem:** Now absorbed by the Mountain Fane, its power is returned to the land. What will this mean for the Wizard of Wines and Barovia's fertility?
*   **Krezk's Fate:** The town is lost, but Marcus's desire to "burn it all down" remains a lingering, unchecked impulse.
*   **Kasimir & the Wagon:** The dusk elf and his resurrected sister, Lazmina, have vanished with the party's wagon. Their whereabouts are unknown.

### **Notable Quotes & Humor**

*   **Aeli**, on choosing a resting place: "It's the best of the terrible options."
*   **Marcus**, with disturbing cheer: "I think we should burn the entire town down. Solves all our problems."
*   **Lars**, on Barovian architecture: "It's like they made the town walls werewolf-proof but forgot the windows."
*   **Aeli**, after Athun's near-fatal bloodletting: "Oh, Thun has to kind of just kill himself now. What the fuck's going on?"
*   **The DM**, on Lars's Reaction: "Jeez, Lars. I give you a free long rest and you complain you had full health already?"
*   The entire **crash-tackle** scene between Aeli and Marcus, stemming from a traumatic fear of glowing magical items.

The session ended on a rare note of tangible hope—the feel of real sunlight—immediately tempered by the grim legend of a blood-bathing witch. The path to Strahd now leads through the swamp, and into the heart of his twisted, familial past.

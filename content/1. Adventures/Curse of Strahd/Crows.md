In the gothic horror setting of *Ravenloft*, particularly in the domain of Barovia, crows are more than just ordinary birds—they are deeply tied to the dark, oppressive atmosphere and the machinations of the land's ruler, Strahd von Zarovich. Here are some facts about crows in Barovia that a player might know, depending on their background, knowledge of nature, or familiarity with Barovia's lore:

---

### 1. **Omens of Misfortune**
   - Crows are often seen as harbingers of doom or bad luck in Barovia. Their presence is considered an ill omen, and their cawing is said to foretell death or tragedy. Locals might avoid areas where crows gather, believing them to be cursed or haunted.

### 2. **Servants of Strahd**
   - Many believe that crows serve as spies for Strahd von Zarovich, the vampire lord of Barovia. It is said that Strahd can see through the eyes of crows, using them to monitor the land and its inhabitants. This makes the birds both feared and reviled by the people of Barovia.

### 3. **Unnatural Behavior**
   - Crows in Barovia often behave in ways that seem unnatural or unsettling. They might gather in unnaturally large flocks, remain eerily silent, or stare at travelers with an almost human-like intelligence. Some say they are not truly birds, but spirits or manifestations of the land's darkness.

### 4. **Symbols of Death**
   - Crows are closely associated with death in Barovia, much like ravens. They are often seen near graveyards, gallows, or sites of recent tragedy. Some Barovians believe that crows carry the souls of the dead to the afterlife—or, more darkly, that they deliver souls to Strahd.

### 5. **Resistant to Harm**
   - Attempting to harm or kill a crow in Barovia is considered extremely dangerous. Locals might warn that the birds are protected by Strahd's magic, and those who attack them could incur his wrath. Some even claim that crows are immortal, reappearing unharmed no matter how many times they are killed.

### 6. **Tied to the Land**
   - Crows are deeply connected to the land of Barovia itself, which is a sentient, malevolent force under Strahd's control. Their behavior often reflects the mood of the land—aggressive crows might signal Strahd's anger, while silent, watchful crows could indicate his curiosity or amusement.

### 7. **Folklore and Superstition**
   - Barovian folklore is rich with tales about crows. Some say they are the spirits of Strahd's past enemies, cursed to serve him for eternity. Others believe that crows can lead lost travelers to safety—or, conversely, lure them into traps set by Strahd or his minions.

### 8. **Contrast with Ravens**
   - While ravens are often seen as more noble or mystical in Barovia (and are sometimes associated with the Keepers of the Feather, a secret society of wereravens), crows are viewed as more sinister and chaotic. The two birds are sometimes seen as opposing forces, with ravens representing hope and resistance, and crows representing despair and subjugation.

### 9. **Crows and the Mists**
   - The mysterious Mists of Ravenloft, which isolate Barovia from other realms, seem to have a strange relationship with crows. Some travelers claim to have seen crows flying into or out of the Mists, leading to speculation that the birds can traverse the boundaries of Barovia—or that they are somehow tied to the Mists themselves.

### 10. **Aesthetic of Barovia**
   - Crows are a constant presence in Barovia's bleak landscape, adding to the domain's oppressive atmosphere. Their black feathers, sharp cries, and watchful eyes contribute to the sense of being constantly observed and hunted, reinforcing the themes of dread and hopelessness that define the setting.

---

These facts would likely be known to players who have spent time in Barovia, spoken with locals, or studied the domain's dark history. However, much about the crows remains shrouded in mystery, leaving room for the Dungeon Master to expand on their role in the story.

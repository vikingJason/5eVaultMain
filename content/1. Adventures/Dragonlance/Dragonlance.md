# What _Is_ Dragonlance?

Imagine a world where the legends are true… but most people have forgotten them.

Dragons vanished centuries ago. The gods abandoned the world after a cataclysm shattered nations. Ancient magic is feared. Knights cling to fading ideals. Ordinary people are trying to survive in a world full of war, propaganda, and uncertainty.

Then the dragons come back.

That’s **Dragonlance**.

At its heart, Dragonlance is:

- Heroic fantasy
    
- Wartime fantasy
    
- A story about hope returning to a broken world
    
- A setting where _choices and ideals matter_
    

It’s less “wandering adventurers looting dungeons for gold,” and more:

> “A group of unlikely heroes standing against the collapse of the world.”

Think:

- _Lord of the Rings_ scale war
    
- Arthurian knights
    
- Ancient prophecies
    
- Magical relics
    
- Tragic heroes
    
- Divine faith returning to the world
    
- Good people trying to stay good during terrible times
    

There’s heroism, but also sacrifice, moral conflict, politics, espionage, ancient evils, and moments of weird whimsical fantasy because this setting absolutely contains:

- Kender accidentally causing international incidents
    
- Gnomes building catastrophically overengineered inventions
    
- Wizards arguing about moon phases like magical accountants
    

Dragonlance can be deadly serious one moment and wonderfully absurd the next.

# The Tone of the Campaign

This campaign starts during the **War of the Lance**.

The world is on the brink of large-scale war, though many people don’t yet realize it.

Your characters are not necessarily “chosen ones” at the start. You are:

- soldiers,
    
- wanderers,
    
- scholars,
    
- mercenaries,
    
- priests,
    
- refugees,
    
- outcasts,
    
- idealists,
    
- criminals trying to do better,
    
- or ordinary people swept into history.
    

The campaign works best when characters:

- care about people,
    
- have strong personal values,
    
- are willing to work together,
    
- and are capable of becoming heroes.
    

Morally grey characters are fine.  
Pure chaos-gremlins who derail emotional moments for memes are probably not.

A good Dragonlance character usually has:

- something they believe in,
    
- something they regret,
    
- and something worth fighting for.

# What Makes Dragonlance Different from Other Fantasy Settings?

## 1. The Gods Matter

For centuries, the true gods have been absent from the world.

Divine magic is rare and mysterious at the beginning of the campaign. People are skeptical of miracles. Religion is fragmented, political, and often corrupted.

If you play a divine character, your powers may feel genuinely miraculous to NPCs.

Faith is a _major theme_ in Dragonlance:

- faith in gods,
    
- faith in ideals,
    
- faith in friends,
    
- and faith that the world can still be saved.
    

---

## 2. Alignment and Ideals Are Important

Dragonlance is one of the few fantasy settings where:

- Good and Evil are cosmic forces,
    
- Order and Chaos are meaningful philosophies,
    
- and your choices can genuinely shape the world.
    

This doesn’t mean:

- “You must play Lawful Good.”  
    It means:
    
- your convictions matter.
    

Characters with strong ideals fit extremely well.

---

## 3. Magic Is Rare, Feared, and Political

Arcane magic is tightly controlled by the Orders of High Sorcery.

Wizards are expected to eventually take _The Test_ — a dangerous magical trial that can permanently change them.

Magic users are respected, feared, and often distrusted.

A wizard in Dragonlance is not just:

> “person who casts Fireball.”

They are part of ancient traditions, magical politics, dangerous secrets, and world-changing power.

---

## 4. Dragons Are a Big Deal

In many fantasy settings, dragons are “Tuesday.”

In Dragonlance:

> Dragons are mythic.

When dragons appear, kingdoms panic.

Dragonarmies reshape the world.

Dragonlance is one of the few settings where dragons feel genuinely apocalyptic.

# Designing a Character for Dragonlance (PF2e Version)

Since we’re converting this into **Pathfinder 2e**, mechanically you’ll have PF2e’s flexibility, but thematically we want characters that _feel_ like they belong in Krynn.

The goal is:

> “PF2e mechanics, Dragonlance soul.”

---

# Character Creation Guidance

## Good Character Themes

These fit Dragonlance extremely well:

### The Idealist

Someone who believes the world can be better.

### The Survivor

A refugee, veteran, or wanderer hardened by loss.

### The Knight

Someone driven by honor, duty, or legacy.

### The Redeemed

Former criminal, mercenary, cultist, or coward trying to become better.

### The Scholar

Interested in lost lore, ancient magic, or forgotten truths.

### The Faithful

Someone searching for meaning, purpose, or the return of the gods.

### The Outsider

A person who doesn’t quite fit anywhere:

- half-elf,
    
- foreigner,
    
- mage,
    
- nomad,
    
- former enemy soldier,
    
- etc.

# PF2e Classes That Fit Especially Well

## Excellent Fits

- Fighter
    
- Champion
    
- Cleric
    
- Wizard
    
- Bard
    
- Ranger
    
- Rogue
    
- Sorcerer
    
- Thaumaturge
    
- Investigator
    
- Swashbuckler
    

## Good With Setting Tweaks

- Witch
    
- Magus
    
- Oracle
    
- Psychic
    
- Monk
    

## Rare or Unusual

Some PF2e options may exist, but should feel uncommon or strange in Krynn:

- Gunslinger
    
- Inventor
    
- Summoner
    
- Androids/clockwork ancestries
    
- Extremely planar/cosmic themed options
    

That doesn’t mean “no.”  
It means:

> we’ll adapt them to fit the setting tone.

Example:

- An Inventor might be tied to eccentric gnomish engineering.
    
- A Psychic may represent moon-linked mystical power.
    
- A Summoner’s eidolon might be tied to ancient dragon spirits.

# Ancestries in Dragonlance

## Common

- Human
    
- Elf
    
- Dwarf
    
- Kender (using Halfling chassis with custom lore)
    
- Half-Elf
    
- Half-Orc (rarer depending on region)
    

## Less Common

- Gnome
    
- Minotaur
    
- Goblin (with adaptation)
    
- Catfolk/Beastkin equivalents in niche regions
    

## Rare

Anything highly exotic should have a strong story reason.

Dragonlance is generally more grounded than baseline Golarion.

---

# The Most Important Thing

Make a character who:

- wants to engage with the world,
    
- has emotional stakes,
    
- and can become part of an epic story.
    

Dragonlance shines brightest when characters:

- form deep friendships,
    
- struggle with difficult choices,
    
- and become symbols of hope in dark times.
    

The setting rewards sincerity.

A character with:

- convictions,
    
- flaws,
    
- loyalties,
    
- fears,
    
- and heart
    

will feel _far_ more Dragonlance than a mechanically optimized build with no connection to the world.

---

# Questions To Help Build Your Character

Consider:

- What does your character believe in?
    
- What would they die protecting?
    
- What do they fear becoming?
    
- How has the war already affected them?
    
- What do they think about the gods?
    
- What do they think about magic?
    
- Who are they trying to prove themselves to?
    
- What would make them become a hero?
    

Or, equally important:

> What would make them fail?

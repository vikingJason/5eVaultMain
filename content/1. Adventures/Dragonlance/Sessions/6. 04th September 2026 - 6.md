## 🧭 Summary
The metallic tang of fear hung heavier over [[Vogler]] than the scent of the sea. Standing at the village gate, the party faced a rider clad in the scales of a nightmare, the messenger of an army that shouldn't exist. [[Mayor Raven Uthvoglar]] held the parchment with trembling hands, the words *Refuse and die* echoing in the silence of the morning.

High on the eastern cliffs flanking the gate, the silhouettes of soldiers appeared—spearmen of the Dragon Army, their weapons glinting with a cold, predatory light. They didn’t attack; they simply watched, a silent promise of the violence to come.

Retreating to the familiar, now stifling walls of the [[Brass Crab]], the air was thick with desperation. [[Cudgel Iron-Smile]], battered but unbowed, slammed a fist onto the table. Her [[Ironclad Regiment]] had been gutted by treachery, leaving only twenty loyal souls to face a thousand. [[Sylvia]], ever the pragmatist, voiced the grim math: they were outmatched, outmanned, and nearly out of time. 

It was [[Becklin Uth-Vaharan]] who broke the deadlock of despair. The veteran knight proposed the unthinkable: the evacuation of [[Vogler]]. The town was a fortress of wood and memories, but it was no match for the "Dragon Queen." The plan took shape—a desperate gambit to stall the messenger, verify the enemy's numbers, and ferry the villagers to [[Kalaman]] via the river.

[[Sylvia]] and [[Silas]] took the most dangerous assignment. To circumvent the guards on the cliffs, they sought out [[Tharn]], a gnome tinker residing in the ruins of [[Thornwall Keep]]. The "tinker" was more of an eccentric architect of chaos, revealing his masterpiece: the Gnome Flinger. It was a catapult designed for people, not stones. With a mixture of terror and resolve, [[Sylvia]] and [[Silas]] strapped into the contraption. [[Tharn]] handed them primitive "parachutes"—canvas wings designed to catch the air—and launched them into the sky.

The flight was a blur of rushing wind and the terrifying realization of their own weight. Below, [[Elara]] and [[Caeden]] watched from the gate, spotting the two figures gliding like clumsy birds over the cliffside. [[Sylvia]] and [[Silas]] landed hard but undetected, shedding their gear and sprinting into the woods to find [[Jeyev]], the mercenary lieutenant who had first spotted the host. 

Deep in the timber, they found him. [[Jeyev]] confirmed the worst: the army was not waiting for nightfall. They were on the march, a sea of red banners and steel just two hours from the gates. 

Back at the village, [[Caeden]] took up the physical burden of defense. The ancient gates of [[Vogler]] had not been closed in a generation; they were sunk deep into the earth, rusted and stubborn. Rejecting the slow work of diplomacy for the grit of a soldier, [[Caeden]] grabbed a shovel. He threw himself into the dirt, digging out the path of the heavy timber doors while [[Elara]] used her [[Natural Medicine]] to mend the party's lingering wounds, her magic a soothing balm against the coming storm.

[[Sylvia]] made one final trip to the keep. She attempted to convince [[Tharn]] to turn his flinger into a weapon of war, but the gnome was a creator, not a killer. He refused to see his "mover" used for carnage, deciding instead to prepare it for destruction so the Dragon Army could never claim his genius. Before leaving, [[Sylvia]] fulfilled a final request from [[Becklin]], retrieving a heavy wooden box from beneath the knight’s bed. She delivered the chest to [[Darret Highwater]] at the docks, passing on [[Becklin]]’s strict instructions: "Open it only once everyone is safe."

As the sun moved toward its zenith, the gates finally groaned shut. [[Caeden]] barred them as best he could, though the original bracing beams had long since rotted away. The plan was set: [[Cudgel]] and her remaining mercenaries would hold the line. [[Sylvia]] prepared bags of caltrops and a desperate plan to set the gate itself ablaze as a final deterrent.

The village was a hive of frantic activity. [[Mayor Raven]] and [[Darret]] organized the flotilla, with [[Atlas]] sent ahead to the docks to act as a signal. Every fisherman, ferryman, and boat-owner in [[Vogler]] readied their craft. They had an hour—maybe less—before the red banners reached the wall. 

The defense of [[Vogler]] was no longer a mock battle on a hill; it was a race against extinction.
